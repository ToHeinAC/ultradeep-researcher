# Interim report: google-tac-magnitude-repricing-and-appellate-tail

**Locus question:** What is Google TAC worth to Apple NOW, how fast does the court's non-exclusive annual-rebidding remedy compress its rate, and how fat is the appellate tail toward a payment ban inside the 2-5 year window?
**Flavor:** dialectical

**Resume note:** this locus was investigated almost to completion by a prior instance whose fetch work survives in the vault (tag `locus-google-tac-magnitude-repricing-and-appellate-tail`) and in `research/runs/apple-earnings-durability-thesis-b8b3f1/temp/scratch-tac/`. That prior work included two exceptionally thorough source-analyst digests of the liability opinion (Doc. 1033) and the remedies opinion (Doc. 1436). This note synthesizes that work, adds the appellate-docket, KGI "Pay for Half," and Alphabet 10-K material the prior instance had fetched but not yet written up, and commits to a position with explicit magnitude/timing numbers. No new fetches were needed — the vault's existing primary-source coverage (liability opinion, remedies opinion, D.C. Circuit docket, DOJ/states cross-appeal brief, economists' amicus brief, KGI remedy papers, Alphabet FY2022/FY2025 10-Ks, Apple's FY2026 Q3 10-Q risk factors) was already sufficient to close all three required deliverables.

## What the corpus already said

The width sweep and prior depth work established the skeleton: Google was found liable in Aug. 2024 for illegally maintaining its search monopoly via exclusive distribution deals, chiefly the Apple ISA [[source-analysis-us-v-google-liability-opinion-doc-1033-apple-isa-economics-and-a]]. Judge Mehta's Sept. 2, 2025 remedies opinion rejected a payment ban and Chrome/Android divestiture but banned *exclusivity* and imposed mandatory annual re-election of defaults across all distribution channels for six years (5+1) [[source-analysis-us-v-google-remedies-opinion-doc-1436-sept-2-2025]]. The contradiction graph (clusters 1–3) already frames the three live fights precisely: (1) toothless-remedy vs. structural-erosion, (2) binary-event vs. repriceable-annuity valuation, (3) whether an alternative bidder puts a floor under Apple's exposure. Secondary commentary is split along predictable lines: Wedbush/Ives and Quartz call it "a monster win for Cupertino" [[how-the-google-antitrust-ruling-reshapes-search-ai-and-more]]; TechPolicy.Press and KGI call it toothless because Google can keep paying and no rival can outbid monopoly-funded offers [[us-courts-remedies-not-enough-given-google-can-continue-to-pay-for-search-defaul]] [[without-a-payment-ban-what-can-we-expect-from-the-us-v-google-data-sharing-remed]]. What none of the width-sweep sources did was (a) put a defended current-dollar range on Apple's TAC, (b) give the appellate docket's actual dates/grounds, or (c) connect the KGI "Pay for Half" proposal's specific numbers to an Apple-relevant dollar impact. That is this note's marginal contribution.

## What the new/synthesized sources say

**Alphabet FY2022 and FY2025 10-Ks** [[goog-20221231]] [[goog-20251231]] give the disclosed trend needed to scale the court's 2022 figure forward. Alphabet discloses total TAC only as an aggregate (distribution partners + Google Network/AdSense partners combined) and as a rate on advertising revenue: "The TAC rate was 22% in both 2021 and 2022" (2022 10-K) against Google advertising revenue of $224,473M, implying total TAC ≈ $49.4B in 2022. The FY2025 10-K: "The TAC rate decreased from 20.7% to 20.3% from 2024 to 2025" against Google advertising revenue of $294,691M, implying total TAC ≈ $59.8B in 2025 (and ≈ $54.8B in 2024 at 20.7% of $264,590M). That is a 21.1% increase in total TAC from FY2022 to FY2025 — a 6.6%/yr CAGR — driven, per the filing's own language, by "growth in TAC paid to distribution partners" even as the *rate* on ad revenue compresses "primarily due to a revenue mix shift from Google Network properties to Google Search & other properties." Neither filing breaks out the distribution-partner-only sub-total or Apple's share — this is a genuine, audited disclosure absence, not an oversight on my part; Google Network (AdSense/AdMob/Ad Manager publisher payments) is a separate, much larger-population bucket blended into the same reported "TAC" line.

**The D.C. Circuit docket** [[usa-v-google-llc-26-5023-courtlistenercom]] gives the appellate posture the query asked for, precisely dated. District court's final judgment: Dec. 5, 2025. Google's notice of appeal: Jan. 16, 2026 (D.D.C.), docketed Jan. 22, 2026 as No. 26-5023 — **Google is Appellant**, challenging (per the docket's own characterization) the data-sharing/syndication remedies and the extension of remedies to GenAI companies — i.e., Google is *not* appealing the payment-continuation/annual-rebidding piece, because it won that piece. DOJ and state AGs' cross-appeals were separately docketed as 26-5047/26-5049 and consolidated with 26-5023 by clerk's order Feb. 5, 2026 — **DOJ/states are Cross-Appellants**, and per curiam scheduling order (April 30, 2026, panel: Walker, Childs, Garcia) set: Google's opening brief due/filed 05/22/2026 (20,787 words); DOJ/states' Appellee-and-Cross-Appellant brief due/filed 07/28/2026 (170 pages); Google's Reply-and-Cross-Appellee brief due 09/29/2026; Deferred Appendix due 10/13/2026; Cross-Appellants' final reply due 10/29/2026. **No oral argument date is set** as of the docket's last update (Sept. 10, 2026).

I read the actual DOJ/states brief text [[oral-argument-not-yet-scheduled]] — filed under the docket entry ironically captioned "ORAL ARGUMENT NOT YET SCHEDULED," this is the government's 170-page Response-and-Cross-Appeal brief. The cross-appeal's question presented is narrow and procedural, not a broadside on Mehta's factual findings: *"Whether the district court erred by rejecting a payment ban without first determining whether a remedy lacking a payment ban would effectively redress Google's violations and restore competition."* The brief's own framing: "the court committed one legal error in rejecting Plaintiffs' request for a ban... Courts have a 'duty' to provide complete and effective relief... but the court never determined whether a payment ban was needed for complete and effective relief." It further quotes Mehta's own words against him: "[d]ue to Google's massive financial advantage and its superior monetization... without a payment ban distributors will be incentivized to stick with Google because it can pay more, thus leaving in place the very forces that effectively have made the ecosystem exceptionally resistant to change" (JA(Rem.Op.127)) — DOJ is using Mehta's own diagnosis against his own remedy choice. Critically, the brief does **not** attack the "harm to distributors"/"windfall to Google" reasoning on the merits; it attacks the *process* by which Mehta reached his conclusion (du Pont "complete and effective relief" doctrine), which matters enormously for what a DOJ win actually produces (see Committed position, below).

**Amicus alignment** [[usa-v-google-llc-26-5023-courtlistenercom]]: Apple filed an "AMICUS FOR APPELLANT [Google]" brief Aug. 25, 2026 (5,696 words) — Apple is actively litigating, in its own name, to preserve the payment-continuation remedy. Samsung (sealed) and Motorola filed alongside Apple, also for Google. On the other side, supporting DOJ/states' cross-appeal: an eight-scholar "Economists and Legal & Technology Scholars" brief (Aug. 4, 2026) [[01208875636-in-usa-v-google-llc-dc-cir-26-5023-courtlistenercom]], the American Antitrust Institute, the American Economic Liberties Project, Public Knowledge, an antitrust professors' group — and, notably, **OpenAI OpCo, LLC and Microsoft Corporation**, both filing to argue for a stronger remedy against the payment status quo. I read the economists' brief directly: it argues "Google's monopoly profits allow it to outbid any rival... no entering competitor will pay more than the monopolist Google to obtain distribution... there is little hope for any new entrants" absent a payment limit, and it explicitly endorses the "Pay for Half" framework as the implementable alternative.

**KGI/Tobin Center "Pay for Half"** [[pay-for-half-a-better-remedy-for-google-search-knight-georgetown-institute]] [[us-v-google-amicus-brief-how-better-remedies-can-restore-competition-knight-geor]] is the one source in the corpus that puts *specific, quantified* numbers on an alternative remedy design, which is exactly what the query's Phase 4 instruction (remedy DESIGN is analysable even when the ruling is not) asks for. Three mechanical components: (1) a **50% market-share cap** — "limit the proportion of devices for which Google can share search revenue to no more than 50% of the devices within any of a distribution partner's product lines"; (2) a **40% payment cap** — "limit the total search revenue Google can pay per device... we suggest a 40% payment cap to start, which can be reduced over time if competition does not emerge"; (3) random device assignment within the paid share, to prevent Google concentrating payments on the highest-value devices. Applied mechanically to Apple, a full "Pay for Half" adoption would (i) render at most half of Apple's device base Google-payment-eligible at all, and (ii) cap the per-device payment rate at a level the paper itself frames as reducible further — a design that, if implemented in full, plausibly cuts Apple's realized TAC dollars by roughly 40–55% relative to whatever the payment would otherwise have been, even before accounting for any competing bidder actually winning share on the freed-up half.

**Apple's own FY2026 Q3 10-K/10-Q risk-factor language** [[aapl-20260627]] confirms the corpus's binary-vs-annuity framing from Apple's own mouth, and is itself an audited absence worth recording: "the Company earns revenue from licensing arrangements with Google LLC... On August 5, 2024, Google was found to have violated U.S. antitrust laws... on September 2, 2025, [the court] ordered certain remedies... The court's order was appealed by both the DOJ and Google. A reversal of the order on appeal could result in imposition of certain remedies initially proposed by the DOJ, such as those prohibiting Google from offering the Company commercial terms for search distribution. If implemented, these remedies could materially adversely affect the Company's ability to earn revenue from such licensing arrangements." Apple discloses the *existence* of the risk in boilerplate, generic terms and gives **no** magnitude, no probability, and no timetable — which is itself the finding: even the party with the best information (Apple, a signatory to the ISA, an active amicus in the appeal) chooses not to quantify this exposure publicly.

## Evidence synthesis

**1. Current TAC magnitude — defended range, method stated.** The only court-anchored figure is Apple's 2022 worldwide payment, "an estimated $20 billion," sourced to Eddy Cue's own sworn testimony and treated as a court finding of fact (FOF ¶299) [[source-analysis-us-v-google-liability-opinion-doc-1033-apple-isa-economics-and-a]]. The court is explicit this is a worldwide, not U.S., figure, and that "Google undoubtedly pays Apple billions of dollars for domestic distribution" without stating how many [[source-analysis-us-v-google-remedies-opinion-doc-1436-sept-2-2025]]. Scaling method: apply Alphabet's disclosed total-TAC growth from FY2022 to FY2025 (6.6%/yr CAGR, computed above from the 10-Ks) to the $20B anchor, holding Apple's share of distribution-partner TAC constant (an explicit, stated assumption — not verifiable from disclosed data, since Alphabet does not break out the distribution-partner sub-total or Apple's share within it in either filing). That yields ≈ $24.2B for FY2025 and, extending one more year at the same rate, ≈ $25.8B for the current (FY2026) run rate. This converges closely with the one available independent estimate in the corpus, Morgan Stanley analyst Erik Woodring's Sept. 2025 note pegging Apple's Google-search revenue at "over $25 billion annually... at 95%-plus margins" [[how-the-google-antitrust-ruling-reshapes-search-ai-and-more]] — an analyst estimate of unstated geography and methodology, explicitly flagged as such, but a useful convergence check on the scaling method rather than an independent confirmation (both numbers ultimately trace back to variants of the same $20B court anchor plus assumed growth). **Defended range: $23B–$29B worldwide for the current (2026) annual run rate, central estimate ≈ $26B**, method = 2022 court figure × Alphabet's FY22→FY25 total-TAC CAGR, holding Apple's distribution-partner share constant, cross-checked against one contemporaneous analyst estimate. A U.S.-only figure cannot be defended to the same standard: no source in the corpus discloses or estimates it, and any number I could construct (e.g., from Apple's ~40% Americas revenue mix and a higher-than-average U.S. search-ad monetization rate) would stack two unverified proxies on top of each other. I flag $10B–$14B as an illustrative, low-confidence U.S.-only band, not a defended estimate at the same confidence tier as the worldwide range — this should be presented in the report as an audited absence with a rough bound, not a point figure.

**2. Rate-path range 2026–2031.** The court record supports direction but not magnitude: both parties' economists agree non-exclusive, annually-recontestable bidding will produce lower payments "in the initial years" (Chipty, plaintiffs' own expert, on cross) and that "with the highest valued customer for your promotion... off the table" rivals would bid less than Google historically overpaid (Murphy, Google's expert) [[source-analysis-us-v-google-remedies-opinion-doc-1436-sept-2-2025]]. The court simultaneously rejected the 31% share-shift estimate as "too speculative," meaning the base-case erosion channel is *price* compression via loss of exclusivity leverage, not *volume* loss. I model two distinct paths because the corpus's own KGI material gives numbers for one of them:

- **Base case (currently-operative remedy holds through 2031, no successful cross-appeal reaching Apple):** gradual downward pressure on the effective revenue-share rate, plausibly low-to-mid-single-digit percentage points of compression per annual re-election cycle, but this operates on the *rate*, not directly on Alphabet's underlying ad-revenue-per-query growth (~6.6%/yr TAC CAGR historically). Net effect: Apple's TAC dollars most likely keep growing in nominal terms through the window, but at a visibly slower CAGR than Alphabet's overall ad-TAC growth — a repriceable annuity with a court-imposed downward bias, not a shrinking one, absent a legal or GenAI-driven shock. On these assumptions I'd bound the 2031 base-case value at roughly $27B–$33B (i.e., materially below what a naive 6.6%/yr extrapolation of the $26B 2026 estimate would give — that naive extrapolation would be ≈ $36B by 2031 — with the shortfall attributable to the repricing drag).
- **Adverse tail (a "Pay for Half"-style cap, or the court's self-reserved payment-ban revisit, is adopted and actually implemented against Apple):** mechanically, per the KGI paper's own two-lever design (50% device-eligibility cap × 40% payment cap, both explicitly capable of being tightened further "if competition does not emerge"), a 40–55% cut to Apple's realized TAC versus the base-case trajectory at the point of implementation. Given the appellate and remand timeline (next section), this is very unlikely to bind before 2028 and more plausibly lands 2029–2031 if it happens inside the window at all — i.e., it is a late-window or beyond-window risk, not a near-term one.

No source in the corpus gives a full annual glide path with named percentages for the base case; the range above is this analyst's construction from the disclosed TAC-growth trend and the qualitative expert-agreement direction, and should be presented in the report as a modeled range, not a disclosed figure.

**3. Appellate posture and reversal-toward-ban likelihood.** See the detailed docket read above. The structurally important fact the query specifically asked me to weigh — **liability attached to de facto EXCLUSIVITY, not the payment mechanism** — is not a Side-A talking point; it is the court's own liability-phase legal holding (Doc. 1033, pp. 209–210, 242): "even though the ISA contains no express exclusivity provision, its terms in combination with Apple's established business practices means that Google will be the only GSE preloaded on an Apple device. That makes it exclusive," and the pre-2005, non-exclusive, revenue-share-free ISA is cited by the court itself as the lawful counterfactual baseline. This bounds what DOJ's cross-appeal can plausibly achieve even in its own best case: because the underlying liability theory targeted exclusivity, not payment size, a full payment ban sits at the outer edge of, arguably beyond, what "fit the wrong" doctrine (Microsoft III) requires — which is precisely why DOJ's cross-appeal is framed as a narrow procedural/legal-standard argument (Mehta failed to make an explicit "necessary for complete relief" finding) rather than a claim that his extensive factual findings on distributor harm and consumer-welfare risk were wrong. That framing matters for outcome, not just optics: the standard remedy for this kind of error is **remand for the district court to make the missing finding**, not a circuit-ordered ban. On remand, Mehta's opinion already telegraphs where he would likely land absent new evidence — his "for now," "prepared to revisit... if competition is not substantially restored" (undated, evidence-triggered, not currently pending), and the explicit footnote-14 "ill-equipped to consider [now]" language on the "unconditional revenue share" middle ground all read as a judge who has already done most of the "is a ban necessary" analysis and simply didn't label it as such — a correctable defect, not obviously an outcome-determinative one.

## Position A (steelman): the remedy is functionally toothless / status-quo-preserving, and the appellate tail is thin

The strongest version of this reading: distributors, including Apple, are expected by the court's own language to keep choosing Google even under full contestability ("[e]ven with newfound flexibility, distributors still are likely to select Google as its primary, if not only, default GSE," remedies op. p.108) [[us-courts-remedies-not-enough-given-google-can-continue-to-pay-for-search-defaul]], because no rival can profitably outbid a firm funding its bids from monopoly ad-pricing profits — a finding the liability opinion itself proves quantitatively via Google's internal "Alice in Wonderland" study (Microsoft would need 122% of Bing's own revenue just to match Google's then-33.75% rate, and "it will not be possible for Alice to match our payments profitably") and via Apple's revealed preference (Microsoft actually offered 100%+ revenue share, even offered to sell Bing outright, and Apple stayed with Google — "I don't believe there's a price in the world that Microsoft could offer us... They could give us the whole company") [[source-analysis-us-v-google-liability-opinion-doc-1033-apple-isa-economics-and-a]]. Annual re-election is real on paper but, per this reading, changes nothing about who wins the bid or roughly how much they pay, because the underlying asymmetry (Google's ad-monetization superiority) that set the price is untouched by a contract-duration rule. On appeal, the government's own cross-appeal is narrow by design — a single legal-error theory, not a facts challenge — and Apple, Samsung and Motorola are actively lobbying the same panel that will decide it to preserve exactly this reading. Stock-market reaction on the ruling day (Alphabet +8.5%, Apple +2.8% "just for keeping its Google annuity intact" [[how-the-google-antitrust-ruling-reshapes-search-ai-and-more]]) is contemporaneous market evidence that sophisticated capital allocators read the remedy the same way in real time.

## Position B (steelman): non-exclusivity plus annual rebidding is a genuine structural break in the pricing mechanism, and the appellate/self-revisit tail is fat enough to matter

The strongest version of this reading does not deny Position A's quality-gap evidence; it says the quality gap governs *volume*, not *price*, and the remedy targets price. Both parties' own economists — plaintiffs' Chipty AND Google's Murphy, an unusual convergence the court itself flags as significant — independently predict lower payments once Google can no longer condition revenue share on multi-year exclusivity: "it is quite possible that in the remedial world payments will be lower. And in the initial years" (Chipty, conceding on cross); "I would expect to see lower payments to partners" with "the highest valued customer for your promotion... off the table" (Murphy) [[source-analysis-us-v-google-remedies-opinion-doc-1436-sept-2-2025]]. The court itself extended the one-year condition to OEMs and carriers *beyond* what Google's own proposal offered, calling contract *duration itself* — not just exclusivity language — anticompetitive, because "[r]ivals cannot presently access [default] channels of distribution without convincing Google's partners to break existing agreements, all of which are binding for a term of years." Separately, and more consequentially than any court order, Apple's own SVP Eddy Cue testified under oath — called as *Google's* witness — that Safari search-query volume declined "for the first time in 22 years," attributed to GenAI chatbot emergence, and that Apple is "actively looking at" AI search alternatives; the court's own stated reason for choosing a shorter six-year term over the plaintiffs' proposed ten was its bet that "technological advancement and product differentiation," not "a decade-long judicial decree," will actually discipline Google. On the appellate tail specifically: this reading notes that Mehta's own words ("distributors will be incentivized to stick with Google... leaving in place the very forces that... made the ecosystem exceptionally resistant to change") are now being quoted back at him by DOJ, that eight independent economists plus Microsoft plus OpenAI have put a concrete, implementable, non-radical alternative (Pay for Half) in front of the same panel, and that a self-reserved, undated revisit trigger sits on Mehta's own docket independent of the appeal's outcome.

## Committed position

The evidence adds up to a **repriceable annuity subject to a court-imposed, one-directional (downward-biased) repricing mechanism, with the appellate legal process as a slow-moving, currently low-probability tail risk and GenAI-driven demand migration as the faster-moving, higher-probability risk the court's own reasoning treats as dominant** — not a binary event, and not a static annuity either. Position B is right about the *mechanism* (price compression via lost exclusivity leverage is real, court-documented, and agreed by both sides' economists) but Position A is right about the *magnitude and near-term speed*: no rival can replace Google's payment at anywhere near comparable value (the "Alice in Wonderland" finding and Apple's revealed 100%+-offer rejection are dispositive on this point, not merely suggestive), so the price compression the remedy induces should be modeled as gradual single-digit-percentage-point annual rate erosion against a growing base, not a step-change loss. On the appellate tail specifically, I read the narrow framing of DOJ's cross-appeal (a procedural "you didn't make the required finding" argument, not a factual challenge to Mehta's distributor-harm findings) plus the exclusivity-not-payment liability theory plus the realistic remand-then-relitigate timeline as together implying a LOW probability (≈15–20%) that a binding payment ban is in effect against Apple's TAC before the end of 2031, a somewhat higher but still LOW-TO-MODERATE probability (≈25–35%) that some intermediate cap along Pay-for-Half or "unconditional revenue share" lines is adopted and binding within the same window, and a MODAL probability (≈45–55%) that the currently-operative remedy (payment continues, exclusivity banned, annual rebidding, gradual price compression only) remains the operative regime for Apple through 2031 essentially unchanged by the appeal. Layered on top of all three legal branches, independent of which one obtains, is the GenAI-substitution risk — which the court's own opinion, not this analyst, identifies as the more consequential threat to the arrangement's economic logic over the horizon, and which is already visibly underway (Safari search-volume decline, Apple's own reported AI-search exploration) in a way no remedy design in this docket addresses.

- **Position:** Current worldwide Apple TAC run-rate is $23B–$29B (central ≈$26B); under the currently-operative remedy it plausibly grows to ~$27B–$33B by 2031 at a CAGR well below Alphabet's own ~6.6%/yr TAC growth (base case, ≈45–55% probability); a Pay-for-Half-style cap cutting realized TAC by 40–55% versus that trajectory is a real but back-loaded tail (≈25–35% probability, unlikely to bind before 2028–2029 even if litigated to a DOJ win, because the cross-appeal's narrow procedural framing most likely produces remand-then-relitigation rather than an appellate-imposed cap); a full payment ban is the least likely branch (≈15–20%) because it sits at or beyond the outer edge of what the exclusivity-based liability theory requires under Microsoft III's "fit the wrong" doctrine.
- **Confidence:** medium. High confidence on the appellate-timeline facts (dates, dockets, briefing schedule, amicus alignment) and on the qualitative direction of price compression (both economists agree; the court's own liability theory is unambiguous on exclusivity-not-payment) — these are primary-document-verified. Medium-to-low confidence on the specific dollar ranges and probability weights, which are this analyst's constructed estimates layered on disclosed anchors, not court- or filing-disclosed figures themselves; Alphabet's non-disclosure of the distribution-partner/Apple sub-split is a real, stated limitation on the magnitude range.
- **Boundary conditions:** the $23B–$29B current-value range and the base-case growth path hold only if (a) Apple's share of Google's distribution-partner TAC has in fact stayed roughly constant since 2022 (unverifiable from disclosed data — Apple could have gained or lost negotiating leverage in the 2021-to-2026 contract term independent of the litigation) and (b) the FY2022–FY2025 total-TAC CAGR is a reasonable proxy for the distribution-partner-only sub-component's growth (plausible given the disclosed mix-shift toward Search & other, but not verified). Outside a scenario where Apple actively exercises its new annual right to solicit competing bids (which the record gives no evidence of yet), the probability weights above should be read as most applicable to the base case continuing largely undisturbed through at least 2027–2028, given the briefing/argument/decision timeline alone rules out a binding legal change before then.
- **What would change this position:** (1) disclosure of Apple's actual current ISA revenue-share percentage (redacted in all court filings reviewed) or of Alphabet's distribution-partner-only TAC sub-total, which would let the magnitude range collapse from a modeled band to a computed figure; (2) a D.C. Circuit oral-argument date or any indication the panel is fast-tracking the case, which would compress the "not before 2028" appellate-timeline judgment; (3) evidence Apple has actually run a competitive annual re-bid (rather than simply retaining Google by default), which would let the base-case erosion range be estimated from a real data point rather than qualitative expert testimony; (4) a materially faster-than-expected Safari/Siri query migration to GenAI assistants (beyond the single "first decline in 22 years" data point already in record), which would shift the dominant risk channel from "legal" to "demand-side" faster than modeled here and would argue for pulling the whole valuation down regardless of the appeal's outcome.
- **Evidence weight:** 2 primary court opinions (liability + remedies, both read in full via source-analyst digests) support the exclusivity-not-payment / alternative-bidder-floor finding directly and heavily; 1 primary appellate docket + 1 full appellate brief (DOJ/states) + 1 full amicus brief (economists) support the appellate-timeline and cross-appeal-scope findings directly; 2 SEC 10-Ks (Alphabet FY2022, FY2025) support the TAC-growth-rate scaling with disclosed, audited numbers; 1 SEC 10-Q (Apple) confirms the risk is real but undisclosed in magnitude by Apple itself; 2 KGI/Tobin policy papers supply the only quantified alternative-remedy design in the corpus; 2 opinion/news pieces (Quartz, TechPolicy.Press) supply the "toothless vs. structural" framing but are commentary, not primary evidence, and are weighted accordingly (used for the steelman sections, not for the magnitude/timeline numbers). No source in the corpus contradicts the exclusivity-not-payment liability finding; the only genuine unresolved empirical disagreement is over *magnitude and speed* of price compression, which neither side's testifying economist quantified in the record I read.

## Open questions

- Apple's actual current (post-2016/2021) ISA revenue-share percentage — redacted throughout the public court record; only the 2016-vintage 33.75% anchor survives. Would sharpen the magnitude range materially if it ever surfaces (e.g., via a future unredacted filing or leaked reporting).
- Alphabet's distribution-partner-only TAC sub-total for FY2024/FY2025 (as opposed to the blended distribution+Network figure disclosed) — not found in either 10-K's text; may exist in an unredacted 10-K footnote or supplemental schedule I did not have budget to search further.
- Whether Apple has exercised, or signaled intent to exercise, its new annual right to solicit competing default-search bids since the remedy took effect (post-Nov. 2025) — no evidence either way in the corpus; this would be the single best real-world data point on actual (vs. modeled) price compression.
- D.C. Circuit oral-argument scheduling once set — the docket note is current only through Sept. 10, 2026; this should be re-checked closer to final report assembly if the pipeline timeline allows, since a surprisingly fast or slow argument date would move the appellate-tail timing judgment.
- Panel judges' (Walker, Childs, Garcia) prior antitrust-remedy jurisprudence — not investigated; could sharpen (or undercut) the probability weights given above, which are currently based only on the structure of the arguments, not judge-specific priors.
