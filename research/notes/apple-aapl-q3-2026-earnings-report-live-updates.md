  * **EPS** : $1.91 adjusted vs. $1.89 estimated

  * **iPad revenue** : $6.19 billion vs. $6.92 billion estimated.

Net income climbed to $29.79 billion, or $2.02 per share, from $23.43 billion, or $1.57 per share, a year ago. Apple said earnings included 11 cents per share from tariff rebates. 
Apple is reckoning with a global memory crunch, or what Cook recently called a "[hundred-year flood](https://www.wsj.com/tech/apple-price-increases-memory-supply-199845b1)," and a rush for chip manufacturing capacity that's already forced the company to lift prices on Macs and iPads. Apple hasn't said it will raise prices on iPhones, but many analysts are expecting hikes as soon as this year.

Revenue growth in the quarter topped 15% for a third straight period.
It was CEO Tim Cook's last earnings call before handing leadership over to John Ternus, a 25-year Apple veteran and the current head of hardware. Ternus said little on the [ call](https://www.apple.com/investor/earnings-call/).

## What Apple is doing about component shortages
After offering soft guidance related to "supply constraints" for parts, Cook explained how Apple is grappling with rising memory costs. It's paying more, and has done so for each of the past three quarters.
"We expect to pay even higher memory costs," Cook said.
Some of that will be offset by the inventory Apple has been holding and by expected savings on non-memory components. 
But Cook doesn't see an end in sight, and said the market should expand beyond its three primary suppliers. 
"If you look beyond September, we see the market pricing for memory continuing to increase, which could drive an increasing impact on our business," Cook said.

## Apple's guidance for the quarter misses expectations 
Apple said revenue growth in the current quarter will be between 9% and 11%, missing analysts' expectations for expansion of 12%, according to LSEG. 
CFO Kevan Parekh said the company continues to see "high levels of demand" for iPhones, with expected sales up in the mid-teens on an annual basis. 
"However, we do expect iPhone revenue to be impacted by these foreign exchange headwinds and supply constraints," Parekh said. 
Apple said it's expecting the foreign exchange piece to hurt growth by 2.5 percentage points in the fiscal fourth quarter. The company said its gross margin will be between 47% and 48%.

## Tariff rebate boosts gross margin over 50%
Apple CEO Tim Cook speaks as U.S. President Donald Trump (L) looks on during an event in the Oval Office of the White House on August 6, 2025 in Washington, DC. 
Win Mcnamee | Getty Images
Apple said tariff refunds added 11 cents to earnings per share, a nice boost for a company that was hit particularly hard by [President Donald Trump's](https://www.cnbc.com/donald-trump/) levies. 
Rebates from U.S. tariffs accounted for two percentage points of additional gross margin, bringing the total margin to a record 50.06%. 
Some of Trump's tariffs were struck down by the Supreme Court earlier this year, allowing companies to seek refunds. Apple was originally hit by higher import fees on finished products and components stemming from the emergency powers act.
Without the rebates, Apple's margin was about in line with estimates.

## Could the memory shortage help Apple gain market share?
Apple CEO Tim Cook and Jalen Brunson, basketball player for the New York Knicks, during the first day of in-store sales of Apple's latest products at Apple's Fifth Avenue store in New York, Sept. 19, 2025.
Bloomberg | Bloomberg | Getty Images
Apple has yet to raise iPhone prices in most markets, which could threaten its margins as costs for memory, storage, and other parts rise. 
But some analysts see Apple picking up share in a dismal smartphone market, potentially the softest since 2013. While Apple is large enough to negotiate large memory supply agreements and take the hit as prices rise, most Android phone makers don't have that luxury and have to pass costs on, potentially losing customers. 
Apple could signal "market share gains given pricing increases at competitors," analysts, who have a buy rating on the stock, wrote in a note this week.
Apple most likely won't announced price hikes on an earnings call, but management often gives data points about "switchers" — people who moved from an Android to an iPhone.

## Apple's capital expenditure-light strategy is paying off in the eyes of investors

VIDEO4:2504:25
Apple is rising because of everyone else's capex plans, says MoffettNathanson's Craig Moffett
Instead of spending hundreds of billions of dollars on AI infrastructure to build and serve advanced models, Apple announced earlier this year that it would license much of its AI technology from Google as well as using its cloud. 
Apple's peers in the hyperscaler world are spending over $100 billion in capex this year, yet Apple is expected to spend just over $11 billion, with $3.4 billion in the June quarter.
In recent weeks, Apple's stock has climbed as many of the names associated with the AI buildout have faltered. 
"While Apple was initially bruised by many investors for not joining the LLM investment cycle, investors are coming around to Apple's industry-leading" free cash flow, analysts at Baird wrote this month. They recommend buying the stock.
