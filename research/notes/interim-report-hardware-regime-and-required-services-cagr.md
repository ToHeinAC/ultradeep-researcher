# Interim report: hardware-regime-and-required-services-cagr

**Locus question:** Is FY2026's hardware-led growth a one-cycle anomaly or a regime change — and what services CAGR is required to hold total growth flat once hardware normalises?
**Flavor:** synthesis

## What the corpus already said

The corpus (10-K/10-Q figures supplied directly to this investigator, not re-fetched) already establishes the shape of the problem: FY2025 Services is 26.2% of revenue ($109,158M of $416,161M) at a 75.4% gross margin against 36.8% for Products — so every point of revenue mix-shift toward Services is worth roughly 2x the margin of a point of Products revenue. FY2026's first three quarters show hardware, not Services, doing the outperforming: iPhone revenue +23.3% (Q1), +21.7% (Q2), +21.7% (Q3) YoY, against Services decelerating through the year (13.9% → 16.3% → 12.1%, guided lower for FQ4 on a 2.5pp sequential FX headwind and chip-node supply constraints). Management attributes the iPhone strength to iPhone 17 demand and flags that Greater China growth was partly a favourable renminbi move, not volume. The replacement-cycle evidence is genuinely disputed: Assurant's trade-in age series (3.16y in 2020 → 3.84y in 2025) and CIRP's share-replacing-a-3+-year-old-iPhone metric (24% in 2020 → 42% in 2025, easing to 39% in Mar 2026) both point to lengthening, but Axis Intelligence's alternative bucket-midpoint derivation of 2.84y shows the dispute is at least partly definitional (mean trade-in age of a self-selected trade-in population vs. a modelled average of the installed base) rather than a clean factual contradiction.

## What the new sources say

**[[idc-smartphone-shipments-up-23-in-q4-2025-apple-is-the-clear-winner-gsmarenacom]]** (IDC Worldwide Quarterly Mobile Phone Tracker via GSMArena, Jan 14 2026) gives the one clean, calendar-aligned unit data point available: Apple shipped 81.3M smartphones in calendar Q4 2025 (+4.9% YoY, vs 77.5M in Q4 2024), overtaking Samsung as #1 vendor for the first time in 14+ years. Calendar Q4 2025 is exactly Apple's FQ1 FY2026 (quarter ended Dec 27, 2025) — the same quarter in which Apple disclosed +23.3% iPhone revenue growth. Full calendar-year 2025: Apple 247.8M units, +6.3% YoY.

**[[idc-smartphone-shipments-to-drop-167-in-2026-as-memory-crisis-bites]]** (IDC blog, Aug 26 2026, Popal/Jeronimo/Reith) is the forward-looking and causal piece: a NAND/DRAM cost shock (+300%+ YoY) is forecast to push global smartphone shipments down 16.7% in CY2026 (steepest ever) while smartphone ASP rises 27.6% to $581 — "the days of the cheap smartphone are ending... consumers are starting to pay the AI bill." Critically, this is **not an Apple-specific or even a premium-tier story**: Android absorbs nearly the entire unit decline (-24.3%), while iOS units are forecast down only -1.3% YoY, with iOS global share rising ~4pp to a record 23.6%. IDC frames the mechanism explicitly as memory-input cost pass-through, not demand pull from AI features, and expects memory prices to stay elevated "at least until 2028." The piece also flags Apple's H2 2026 foldable entry (>17M units by 2027, ASP >$2,550) as a distinct, non-mix-neutral upside that is orthogonal to the "replacement cycle" question.

**[[apple-iphone-sales-by-quarter-fy-q3-2007-q1-2021-dazeinfo]]** and **[[apple-revenue-2012-2026-aapl-macrotrends]]** together are the only sources with enough historical span to reconstruct supercycle decay. Dazeinfo confirms the FQ1 FY2021 iPhone-12 peak at 90.1M units (+22.2% YoY) but — its own text explicitly says — carries no extractable unit figures for the iPhone 6 (FY2015) or iPhone X (FY2018) launch quarters, only qualitative language ("unprecedented," "best-selling model"). Macrotrends supplies the complete quarterly *total-company* revenue series 2011–2026, which is the only way to reconstruct the iPhone-6 and iPhone-X cycles at all (see decay table below); it is a secondary aggregator cross-checked against Apple's own filings but is not itself iPhone-segment data.

**[[cirp-first-time-iphone-buyers-dont-feel-the-need-to-buy-the-latest-greatest-mode]]** (CIRP via MacTech, Jul 2026) adds a mix-drag detail: in the six months to March 2026, only 68% of Android switchers bought a current flagship (17/17 Air/17 Pro/17 Pro Max) vs 80% of upgrading legacy iPhone owners. Switchers are ~12% of iPhone buyers in the window. This works against the ASP-inflation thesis at the margin — a growing switcher cohort buys down the lineup — but is a second-order effect next to the memory-driven, industry-wide ASP shock IDC documents.

**[[apple-valuation-faces-scrutiny-as-services-growth-cools]]** (Tradingpedia, Aug 14 2026) is the sell-side framing source and contains a **direct numeric conflict with the orchestrator-verified figures**: it states Services revenue "slipped below 10% year-over-year for the first time since the June 2023 quarter" for the same quarter ($30.74B) that the verified figure gives as $30,739M, +12.1% YoY. Both cite the identical dollar figure ($30.74B ≈ $30,739M) for what must be the same quarter (Q3 FY2026), so this is not a different quarter — it is a growth-rate claim built on a different or miscalculated YoY base (possibly reported growth vs. a constant-currency or estimate-miss framing conflated with YoY growth in the article's own prose). Given the source hierarchy this run applies (10-Q disclosure > analyst-commentary aggregator), the verified 12.1% should be treated as correct and the "<10%" claim as a probable reporting/definitional error, not a genuine second data point — worth flagging explicitly because the article's own bear case (KeyBanc's "price-led growth deserves a lower multiple," Barclays $230 PT) is being built partly on that shakier number. The article's other content is useful and consistent with other corpus notes: Morgan Stanley cut its price target to $360 explicitly citing slower Services expansion, while simultaneously raising its FY26 iPhone shipment forecast to 243M units (+3.3% YoY) — i.e., even the Services bear is not calling the iPhone cycle over.

## Evidence synthesis

**1. Units-vs-ASP decomposition of FY2026 iPhone revenue growth (bounded estimate, one clean quarter only).** The only calendar-aligned pairing of Apple-specific IDC units against Apple's own disclosed iPhone revenue is FQ1 FY2026 (Oct–Dec 2025 = calendar Q4 2025): IDC units +4.9% YoY vs Apple iPhone revenue +23.3% YoY. Implied ASP/mix growth = (1.233/1.049) − 1 ≈ **+17.5%**. On this one quarter, roughly 4.9 points of the 23.3-point revenue growth came from volume and roughly 17.5 points (relative, i.e. the large majority of the growth) came from price/mix. **This decomposition cannot be cleanly extended to FQ2 or FQ3 FY2026** — the corpus contains no IDC calendar-Q1-2026 or calendar-Q2-2026 Apple-specific unit release; this is an audited absence, not an oversight (searched the vault for "IDC Apple shipments Q1 2026" and "Q2 2026" specifically; nothing found). The best bounded forward read is IDC's CY2026 forecast that iOS units fall only -1.3% for the full calendar year against an industry-wide ASP rise of 27.6% — consistent with the same pattern (price, not volume, carrying growth) continuing through the year, but this is an IDC forecast, not a released actual, and applies to "iOS" as a category, a reasonable but imperfect proxy for Apple's own unit count.

**2. Historical decay of prior iPhone supercycles (total-company quarterly revenue, YoY, as proxy — iPhone-segment quarterly revenue is not available for FY2015/FY2018 in this corpus).**

| Supercycle | Peak qtr (YoY growth) | Q+4 | Q+5 | Q+6 | Q+7 | Q+8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone 6 (FQ1 FY2015) | +29.5% | +1.7% (FQ1 FY16) | **-12.8%** | **-14.6%** | -9.0% | +3.3% |
| iPhone X (FQ1 FY2018) | +12.7% | **-4.5%** (FQ1 FY19) | **-5.1%** | +1.0% | +1.8% | +8.9% |
| iPhone 12 (FQ1 FY2021) | +21.4%* | +11.2% (FQ1 FY22) | +8.6% | +1.9% | +8.1% | -5.5%** |

\*iPhone-12-cycle Q+1 to Q+3 are inflated by 2020 COVID-lockdown low base comps (+53.6%, +36.4%, +28.8%) and should not be read as pure demand strength.
\*\*Q+8 for the iPhone-12 cycle (FQ1 FY2023, -5.5%) is confounded by the Dec-2022 Zhengzhou plant COVID disruption — a supply shock, not demand decay — so it likely overstates the cycle's "natural" hangover.

Reading the three rows together: the iPhone-6 cycle produced the most violent hangover (two quarters of double-digit revenue *declines*, driving Apple's first-ever revenue-decline fiscal year, FY2016). The iPhone-X cycle produced a milder but still negative hangover (two quarters of -4.5%/-5.1%, the Jan-2019 Tim Cook shareholder letter cycle) before recovering to flat-to-positive by Q+6. The iPhone-12 cycle, notably, stayed in positive territory through Q+7 and only turned negative at Q+8 under an identifiable supply-shock confound — the mildest post-peak hangover of the three, once COVID base effects and the Zhengzhou disruption are set aside. Each successive supercycle's hangover has been progressively less severe. That pattern is consistent with — though this corpus cannot fully isolate the causal share of — a rising Services mix cushioning total-company growth even as the hardware component decays, exactly the mechanism the query's "required services CAGR" question is asking about.

**3. Required Services CAGR** (formula: s = [(1+g)(P0+S0) − (1+p)P0]/S0 − 1, P0=$307,003M, S0=$109,158M):

| Target total growth g | Products growth p = 0% | p = -3% | p = -8% |
|---|---|---|---|
| g = 0% (flat) | s = 0.0% | s = 8.4% | s = 22.5% |
| g = 5% | s = 19.1% | s = 27.5% | s = 41.6% |

Inverted — the more decision-relevant framing — solving for the **breakeven Products growth p that flat total revenue (g=0%) tolerates**, given Services actually growing at its observed FY2022–FY2026 rates (12.1%, 14.2%, 16.3%):

| Observed Services growth s | Breakeven Products growth p (for g=0%) | Breakeven p (for g=5%) |
|---|---|---|
| 12.1% (FQ3 FY2026, weakest recent quarter) | **-4.3%** | +2.5% |
| 14.2% (FY2022 actual) | **-5.1%** | +1.7% |
| 16.3% (FQ2 FY2026, strongest recent quarter) | **-5.8%** | +1.0% |

Plausibility read: at Services' actually-observed 12–16% growth band, total revenue can stay flat even if Products/hardware revenue *declines* by roughly 4–6% a year — a cushion of almost exactly the magnitude of the iPhone-X-cycle hangover (-4.5%/-5.1% in FQ1/FQ2 FY2019 above). Services at its current run rate is therefore **historically sufficient to absorb an iPhone-X-severity hangover but not an iPhone-6-severity one** (-12.8%/-14.6%): the s required to offset a -8% Products year (22.5%) is nearly 40% above the best quarter Services has posted in this corpus (16.3%) and has no precedent in the FY2022–FY2026 window. For any *positive* total-growth target (g=5%), the math is much less forgiving: Services at 12–16% growth only tolerates Products growth as low as +1% to +2.5% — i.e., no outright hardware hangover at all, just deceleration to low-single-digits. Put plainly: Services can keep Apple's total revenue from shrinking through a moderate hardware correction, but it cannot by itself deliver above-zero total growth if hardware actually contracts.

## Committed position

FY2026 is best read as a **hybrid, not a clean anomaly-or-regime-change binary**: the growth composition (units +4.9% vs. revenue +23.3% in the one quarter this corpus can cleanly decompose) shows this is overwhelmingly a price/ASP cycle, not a unit-driven "supercycle" in the iPhone-6/iPhone-12 sense — and the price inflation is substantially exogenous (IDC's memory-cost shock, expected to persist to at least 2028, hitting the entire industry, not an Apple-specific AI-driven upgrade pull). That means research question 9's framing needs correcting: there is no clean evidence in this corpus of an "AI-driven upgrade supercycle" resetting the replacement cycle — the CIRP/Assurant data show the replacement cycle continuing to *lengthen* (3.16y→3.84y) through the same period FY2026's revenue is accelerating, and the switcher-mix evidence (CIRP) if anything works against an upgrade-driven story. What is resetting is smartphone-industry unit economics (memory scarcity), which happens to flatter Apple disproportionately because Apple's premium positioning and IDC's forecast -1.3% iOS unit decline (vs -24.3% for Android) let it pass costs through without losing share — a market-structure effect, not a demand-cycle effect. On the required-services-CAGR question: the arithmetic shows Services' current 12–16% growth is a real but *bounded* shock absorber — sufficient to keep total revenue flat through a hangover on the order of the 2019 iPhone-X correction (-4% to -6% Products growth) but not through a 2016 iPhone-6-style double-digit hangover, and nowhere near sufficient to deliver positive total growth (g=5%) if hardware genuinely contracts. Combined with the historical decay table's finding that each successive supercycle's hangover has gotten milder — plausibly because Services' rising mix share has been doing exactly this cushioning job for a decade — the query's "hardware maturity" premise holds directionally over 2–5 years (a mean-reverting FQ4 FY2026 guide of only 9–11% total growth against FQ1–Q3's 16–17% is itself evidence of deceleration already starting), but the mechanism is not "AI supercycle vs. structural lengthening," it is "temporary industry-wide ASP shock layered on top of a structurally lengthening replacement cycle, with Services providing a real but quantifiably limited floor under total-revenue growth."

- **Position:** FY2026's hardware strength is a price/ASP-driven, largely exogenous (memory-cost) cycle layered on top of a structurally lengthening iPhone replacement cycle, not an AI-driven demand reset; Services' 12-16% current growth rate is sufficient to keep Apple's total revenue from shrinking through a hangover of iPhone-X (2019) severity (-4% to -6% Products growth) but not through an iPhone-6 (2016) severity hangover (-8% or worse), and cannot deliver positive (5%) total growth if hardware genuinely contracts.
- **Confidence:** medium. The units-vs-ASP decomposition rests on one clean calendar-aligned quarter (FQ1 FY2026); the historical decay table uses total-company revenue as an iPhone proxy because iPhone-segment quarterly data for FY2015/FY2018 was not available in the source budget; and the Q+8 iPhone-12 datapoint is supply-shock confounded.
- **Boundary conditions:** the required-services-CAGR arithmetic is a static, single-year mechanical decomposition (P0/S0 fixed at FY2025 actuals) — it says nothing about margin mix, multiple compression, or what growth rate the market will actually reward (the Tradingpedia source's KeyBanc thesis — that price-led growth deserves a lower multiple than volume-led growth — is a separate, valuation-layer question this locus does not adjudicate). The historical-decay reading holds for Apple's specific demand pattern under three well-documented past cycles; it does not extrapolate mechanically to a fourth cycle with a different (input-cost, not demand) origin.
- **What would change this position:** (a) an IDC or Counterpoint release giving calendar-Q1/Q2-2026 Apple-specific unit shipments — if that showed units accelerating in step with revenue (not just Q4 2025's 4.9%), the "price-not-volume" reading would need softening; (b) an FQ4 FY2026 print showing Services growth reaccelerating above 18-20% would materially raise the "cushion" ceiling and weaken the "bounded absorber" conclusion; (c) confirmation that IDC's memory-cost shock resolves faster than 2028 (e.g., a NAND/DRAM price collapse in 2027) would reclassify this cycle as more clearly transient rather than a hybrid structural/cyclical story.
- **Evidence weight:** 1 clean quarter of Apple-specific unit-vs-revenue decomposition (IDC via GSMArena) supports the ASP-led reading; 1 forward-looking IDC industry forecast (memory shock, iOS -1.3% vs Android -24.3%) supports the exogenous/market-structure framing; 3 historical supercycles reconstructed from total-company revenue (a proxy, not iPhone-segment data) support the "hangovers getting milder over time" pattern; 1 CIRP data point weakly counters the "AI supercycle" framing; 1 analyst/valuation source (Tradingpedia) contains an internally-inconsistent Services growth figure that this note resolves in favor of the verified 12.1% filing-based number.

## Open questions

- No IDC or comparable third-party unit-shipment release for calendar Q1 2026 or calendar Q2 2026 specifically for Apple was found in this corpus — searched and confirmed absent. This is the single biggest gap preventing a full four-quarter units-vs-ASP decomposition of FY2026.
- No iPhone-segment (as opposed to total-company) quarterly revenue series for FY2015 (iPhone 6) or FY2018 (iPhone X) was available within the source budget — the decay table above is a total-company-revenue proxy. If iPhone-segment 10-Q quarterly figures for those years were pulled directly (out of scope here per the resume-job note-body-size restriction), the decay percentages would likely be somewhat larger in magnitude (iPhone was a bigger share of revenue then) but the qualitative pattern (violent iPhone-6 hangover > moderate iPhone-X hangover > mild iPhone-12 hangover) is unlikely to reverse.
- Whether the FQ4 FY2026 guide (9-11% total growth) ultimately lands nearer 9% or 11%, and how much of the gap is FX vs. actual hardware/services deceleration, is a live open question this locus cannot resolve pre-print.
