summary: 'Primary source: full text of the Ninth Circuit''s December 11, 2025 published
  opinion (No. 25-2935, Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc., Judge Milan D. Smith Jr. writing,
  argued Oct 21 2025) affirming in part and reversing in part the district court''s
  April 30, 2025 civil-contempt order. Confirms Apple''s link-out commission was set
  at 27% (30% IAP commission minus a 3% ''cost of payments discount'' Apple itself
  judged too small to spur adoption) and that the panel found ''charging a 27% commission
  had a prohibitive effect, in violation of the injunction.'' Documents Apple''s ''scare
  screen'' (bold warning that a link leads to an ''external website'' that ''sounds
  scary,'' displaying the developer''s name to intensify the warning) as a further
  violation. Vacates the district court''s complete ban on any linked-out commission
  as overbroad: Apple ''should be able to charge a commission on linked-out purchases
  based on costs that are genuinely and reasonably necessary for its coordination
  of external links...but no more'' (the ''necessary costs'' standard), with ''some
  compensation for the use of its intellectual property'' allowed. On remedies, the
  panel modifies the injunction to let Apple require parity in size/prominence/placement
  (developer links may not be more prominent than Apple''s own) while remanding to
  the district court to (i) address the VPP/NPP developer exclusion issue and (ii)
  recast the commission prohibition as either a purgeable contempt sanction or a properly
  tailored injunction modification. Separately affirms denial of Apple''s request
  to vacate the underlying injunction under Trump v. CASA and denies Apple''s request
  for reassignment to a different judge. Disposition: ''AFFIRMED in part, REVERSED
  in part, and REMANDED.'''

ANALYSIS    The district court found Apple in contempt for violatingthe Injunction, and in the April 30 Order, it issued specificrestraints on Apple’s conduct pursuant to the Injunction.The district court did not abuse its discretion by findingApple in contempt. The district court’s restrictions in theApril 30 Order are affirmed in part and reversed andremanded in part. Despite Apple’s arguments to thecontrary, most of the restrictions imposed by the districtcourt align with the Injunction, although they are overbroadin some respects. However, the commission prohibition isnot an appropriately cabined civil contempt sanction.Accordingly, the April 30 Order is reversed in relevant partand remanded to the district court. CONCLUSION     We modify the April 30 Order as follows: (i) where bothApple and a developer offer a purchase option, Apple mayrestrict the developer from placing its buttons, links, or othercalls to action in more prominent fonts, larger sizes, largerquantities, and more prominent places than Apple uses forits own buttons, links, or other calls to action; (ii) Apple mayrestrict developers from using language that violates itsgeneral content standards, if such standards exist; (iii) Appleis not specifically enjoined from excluding developersparticipating in the VPP and NPP programs from54               EPIC GAMES, INC. APPLE INC.simultaneously obtaining link access; and (iv) Apple is notenjoined from imposing a commission or fee on purchasesthat consumers make in an app utilizing iOS outside theApple Store (a linked-out purchase) as permitted by thedistrict court on remand. The Injunction and April 30 Orderotherwise remain in full effect.    We remand to the district court for the following: (i) toconsider whether Apple’s exclusion of VPP and NPPdevelopers violated the Injunction, or whether it wasnecessary to protect or give life to the Injunction; and (ii) toamend the April 30 Order’s commission prohibition as eithera purgeable civil contempt sanction or properly tailoredclarification or modification of the Injunction.
