summary: 'FRED series DFII10 (10-year US Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities yield,
  i.e. the real 10-year rate, daily, not seasonally adjusted, source: Federal Reserve
  Board H.15 release). CURRENT LEVEL AS OF FETCH (Sept 13, 2026): most recent observation
  2026-09-10 = 2.55%. Recent daily trajectory: 2026-09-04 = 2.43%, 2026-09-08 = 2.43%,
  2026-09-09 = 2.46%, 2026-09-10 = 2.55% -- roughly a 12-basis-point rise concentrated
  in the same Sept 9-10 window as the nominal-yield jump seen in [[market-yield-on-us-treasury-securities-at-10-year-constant-maturity-quoted-on-an]]
  (DGS10). PAIRED WITH NOMINAL: nominal 10Y = 4.95% and real 10Y = 2.55% on 2026-09-10
  implies a 10-year breakeven inflation rate of approximately 2.40 percentage points
  on that date (4.95% - 2.55%). Because the real yield rose almost as much as the
  nominal yield in this window (12bp vs 17bp), the move looks like a genuine real-rate/discount-rate
  shift rather than purely a change in inflation expectations -- directly relevant
  to equity-duration arguments, since it is the REAL rate (the pure-discounting-adjacent
  component per Gormsen & Lazarus, see [[interest-rates-and-equity-valuations]]) that
  should matter most for long-duration equity valuations.'
