# Audited absence: no published adoption-rate figure for EU alternative iOS app marketplaces

## What was searched

Targeted search for a measured percentage/rate of EU iPhone users who have downloaded, installed, or actively use a third-party ("alternative") iOS app marketplace under the DMA (AltStore PAL, Aptoide, Epic Games Store on iOS, Setapp Mobile, Skich). Sources checked:

- Apple's own commissioned DMA study (Analysis Group / Jane Choi, Nov 2025) — measures price pass-through of the alternative *business terms* fee schedule, explicitly excludes marketplace usage (footnote: "This study is limited to transactions on the App Store and does not analyze prices on the web or other potential alternative marketplaces").
- TechCrunch's Feb 2026 marketplace catalog and Jan 2026 Setapp Mobile shutdown report — both purely descriptive, zero usage/download figures.
- European Commission DMA non-compliance and specification decisions (19 Mar 2025 Article 6(7) interoperability specification decisions; 23 Apr 2025 Article 5(4) anti-steering non-compliance decision, EUR500m fine, published 6 Jun 2025) — address compliance and remedies, not usage statistics.
- Analyst/market-research queries against Sensor Tower and Appfigures via general web search — no published usage-share report for EU alternative iOS marketplaces was located.
- AltStore's own disclosures (Oct 2025 $6M Series A from Pace Capital) — discloses "100+ developers on board" and 100+ self-published apps, no user/install count.

## Conclusion

**No official (European Commission), academic, or named-analyst-firm figure exists, as of this search (September 2026), giving the percentage or absolute count of EU iPhone users who have adopted an alternative iOS app marketplace.** This appears to be a genuine, currently unfilled data gap — the Commission's own compliance decisions do not report a usage metric, and no analytics vendor has a public report indexed by search engines.

## Best available proxies (not adoption *rates*, but bound the plausible magnitude)

1. **Epic Games Store mobile (self-disclosed, Jan 2025):** 29 million installs since August 2024 launch, aggregated across Android + iOS and across every country where EGS mobile had launched (EU plus initial rollout markets — NOT EU-only, NOT iOS-only) — against Epic's own target of 100 million by end of 2024 (a 71M miss). Epic separately discloses an EU-specific friction metric: "an additional 5 million attempts to install the Epic Games Store were blocked by Apple's browser and operating system 'Eligibility Requirements'" in the EU, and states more than 50% of install *attempts* globally fail due to Apple/Google "scare screen" flows. No denominator (total EU iPhone install base) is given, so no rate can be derived — but the absolute numbers are small relative to Apple's ~150M+ estimated EU iPhone install base, consistent with low single-digit-percent adoption at most, though this is an inference, not a disclosed figure. [note: app-store-economy-is-far-from-open-despite-efforts-by-epic-developers-and-regula]
2. **Setapp Mobile exit (Feb 2026):** MacPaw shut down its EUR9.99/month subscription alternative marketplace after ~17 months, citing business-model non-viability under Apple's fee structure — evidence that at least one alternative-marketplace bet failed to reach viable scale, independent of any measured adoption shortfall. [note: one-of-the-first-alternative-app-stores-in-the-eu-is-shutting-down-techcrunch]
3. **Zero of the top 100 highest-grossing mobile game developers** were willing to list on the Epic Games Store as of Jan 2025 because of Apple's Core Technology Fee — evidence that *supply-side* migration to alternative marketplaces has also been minimal, which independently caps how much App Store commission revenue can migrate away regardless of consumer-side adoption. [note: app-store-economy-is-far-from-open-despite-efforts-by-epic-developers-and-regula]
4. **Apple's own commissioned study (Nov 2025)** found developers who did switch to lower-commission *terms within the App Store itself* (not even a third-party marketplace) passed savings to consumers only 9% of the time — a directionally separate but related data point suggesting even where commission erosion clearly occurred (verified: EUR20.1M in EU commission savings over ~6 months), 86% of the benefit flowed to non-EU developers, not EU consumers, and Apple's App Store remained the transaction venue. [note: what-happens-to] (Apple-funded; treat as biased toward showing the DMA regime doesn't help consumers, but the underlying transaction-level facts — EUR20.1M/41M transactions/~21,000 products — are less likely to be fabricated than the framing.)

## Implication for the report's erosion-speed bound (§4.3/§6.3/§7.1)

The absence of a measured adoption rate is itself informative for a 2-5 year holding view: if EU regulators, Apple (adversarially incentivized to show DMA "isn't working"), and Epic (adversarially incentivized to show Apple is blocking adoption) all lack or withhold a clean adoption-rate figure nearly two years after the DMA's alternative-marketplace mandate took effect (March 2024) and roughly 18 months after Epic Games Store's EU iOS launch (August 2024), the most defensible inference is that adoption has been low enough that no party finds a hard percentage worth publishing — Apple would publish a low number to underscore "consumers don't want this," and no interested party has. Combined with proxy #1 (double-digit millions of installs against a stated 100M target, spread across multiple countries and platforms) and proxy #2 (a marketplace operator exiting on commercial-viability grounds within 17 months), the evidentiary record supports treating EU alternative-marketplace-driven App Store commission erosion as SLOW over a 2-5 year horizon — bounded in the low single digits of percentage points of EU App Store revenue per year at most, not a step-function threat — while flagging that this is an inference from proxies and an audited absence, not a disclosed rate, and should be labeled as such in the report rather than presented as a point estimate.
