BOM for the iPhone X estimated at $409.25, of which Chinese firms contribute $104 (25.4%) — up sharply from just $6.50 (assembly-only) for the 2009 iPhone 3G. Of the $1,000 retail price, Chinese firms capture only 10.4% of total value added. Ten domestic Chinese firms are identified in iPhone X production (e.g., Sunwada supplying the battery pack; Dongshan Precision supplying PCBs via its acquired US subsidiary M-Flex), moving beyond pure assembly into 'relatively sophisticated' but still non-core segments. Using value-added rather than gross trade value, the US-China bilateral deficit attributable to one iPhone X is $104, not the $332.75 implied by gross export value ($409.25 minus $76.5 of US-sourced parts) — because Korea, Japan and other countries jointly supply over 45% of components, and gross accounting misattributes their value-added to China. Applying this to 2017's 42.2 million US iPhone imports, gross-value trade statistics overstated the true US-China iPhone deficit by an estimated $9.65bn (~2.3% of total 2018 bilateral deficit). Xing further shows yuan depreciation cannot offset Trump-era tariffs on the iPhone because so much of its value-added is non-Chinese: a 25% tariff on the iPhone X (levied on the full $409.25 base) would require an 'unthinkable 400%' yuan depreciation to fully offset, versus 43.4% for the average Chinese export bundle (33.9% foreign value-added). Notes that by 2019 Apple had already asked major suppliers to evaluate shifting 15-30% of production capacity from China to Southeast Asia (citing Nikkei, June 2019) — evidence that diversification planning began years before the 2025 tariff escalation, useful for dating how long execution has actually taken. `review`

Method: total landed cost of a China-assembled iPhone 16 = $549.73 in components (sourced from Taiwan, South Korea, China, US, Japan, plus an estimated 8-10 further subcomponent-origin countries — Ross (2020) is cited estimating up to 43 source countries total) plus $14.00 paid to Foxconn for final assembly, totaling $563.73. On tariff mechanics: because tariffs are typically assessed on the full FOB value assembled-in-China ($563.73) rather than on the $38.89 of actual Chinese value-added, the effective tariff burden vastly overstates the economic 'China content' being taxed — a structural argument for why value-added-based tariff refunds/rebates are complex to administer ('an accounting nightmare for both the company and the customs authorities').
