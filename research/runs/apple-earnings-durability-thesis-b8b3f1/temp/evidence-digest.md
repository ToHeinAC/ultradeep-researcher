## Top contested claim pairs (from the contradiction graph)

Both sides with verified quotes, chosen by topical overlap with each side's position. Where no verified claim matched, the side lists its source notes only. Committed resolutions live in source-tensions.json and comparisons.md.

### Does the US v. Google remedy leave Apple's ~$20B search-placement revenue effectively intact, or does non-exclusivity plus annual rebidding structurally erode it?

**Side A:** Remedy is toothless: payments continue, Apple keeps Google, nothing material changes.

- Judge Mehta's Sept 2, 2025 remedies opinion in US v. Google does not ban Google's default search payments to Apple, Samsung, and Mozilla, only bars exclusive distribution deals.
  > The judge has allowed these default payments to continue based on his expectation that generative AI companies will exert competitive pressure on Google Search in the future, and out of concern that Google's search distributors would suffer without default payments from the monopolist, while barring Google from striking exclusive distribution deals.
  [[without-a-payment-ban-what-can-we-expect-from-the-us-v-google-data-sharing-remed]]

- Google's stock price rose nearly 8% on September 2, 2025, the day the remedies ruling was issued, reflecting market interpretation of the outcome as a win for Google (and by extension, favorable for Apple's continued TAC revenue).
  > Markets quickly interpreted the outcome as a win for Google, with its share price rising by nearly 8% after the decision.
  [[google-antitrust-ruling-key-takeaways-from-the-district-courts-decision-publicat]]

**Side B:** Non-exclusivity and one-year terms change the PRICING mechanism and erode the rent over time.

- Browser Developer agreements (covering Apple/Safari) must allow the developer to annually set a different general search engine at various access points across devices, as a condition of receiving Google's revenue share.
  > Google also would be permitted to pay Browser Developers, including Apple, to set Search as the default GSE, so long as the Browser Developer (1) can promote other GSEs and (2) is permitted to set a different GSE on different operating system versions or in a privacy mode and makes changes, if desired, on an annual basis.
  [[united-states-district-court-2]]

- The remedies ruling bans exclusive search distribution deals, giving distribution partners like Apple leverage to feature competing search engines and renegotiate terms.
  > The court did not ban Google's payments to partners such as Apple to set Google as the default search engine, but it did prohibit exclusive search distribution deals. This ruling enables a variety of companies, including browser providers and cellphone and laptop manufacturers, to feature other search engines and gives them greater leverage in negotiations with Google.
  [[court-issues-remedies-ruling-in-united-states-v-google-search-case-hughes-hubbar]]

**Proposed resolution:** The fee is durable in existence and default choice, fragile in rate. Model as an annuity with a declining rate path, not as survive/die.

**Side A:** Binary: a single court or appellate outcome can void the stream.

- _No verified quote matched this position; sources:_ [[aapl-20250927]], [[aapl-20260627]], [[google-antitrust-ruling-key-takeaways-from-the-district-courts-decision-publicat]], [[apple-explores-move-to-ai-search-in-browser-amid-google-fallout-bloomberg]]

**Side B:** Repriceable annuity: the existence question has resolved in Apple's favour; the live variable is the rate.

- _No verified quote matched this position; sources:_ [[united-states-district-court]], [[united-states-district-court-3]], [[source-analysis-us-v-google-remedies-opinion-doc-1436-sept-2-2025]], [[source-analysis-us-v-google-liability-opinion-doc-1033-apple-isa-economics-and-a]], [[without-a-payment-ban-what-can-we-expect-from-the-us-v-google-data-sharing-remed]]

### If Google's payment shrinks, could an alternative bidder (Microsoft, OpenAI, Perplexity) replace it — i.e. is there a floor under Apple's exposure?

**Side A:** Yes: non-exclusive annual auctions let Apple play bidders against each other.

- _No verified quote matched this position; sources:_ [[how-the-google-antitrust-ruling-reshapes-search-ai-and-more]]

**Side B:** No viable floor: no rival can offer comparable value because no rival has comparable search quality.

- Bing's search index is roughly one-fifth the size of Google's yet achieves comparable search quality on desktop, per the UK CMA's 2020 investigation, suggesting index-size scale advantage is not an absolute barrier to a viable alternative bidder.
  > Bing can do this with a search index that is about a fifth of that of Google's (see para 75, CMA 2020, Appendix I: search quality and economies of scale).
  [[us-courts-remedies-not-enough-given-google-can-continue-to-pay-for-search-defaul]]

- 93% of search queries in a sampled query log were seen only by Google, versus about 5% seen only by Bing, illustrating the long-tail data moat that alternative bidders would have to overcome to bid comparably for default placement.
  > The Plaintiffs' expert examined a query log sample and found that 93% of the queries were seen only by Google, whereas about 5% were seen only by Bing.
  [[without-a-payment-ban-what-can-we-expect-from-the-us-v-google-data-sharing-remed]]

**Proposed resolution:** There is no meaningful floor. This makes the rate-compression mechanism MORE consequential: Apple's bargaining leverage is weak AND Google's incremental value from a non-exclusive default is lower (users route to Google anyway). Both point to rate compression without replacement.

### Will AI relocate the user interface away from the smartphone within the 2-5 year window, eroding Apple's distribution advantage?

**Side A:** Yes: AI threatens to make the device secondary.

- Eddy Cue's May 2025 testimony that AI may make the iPhone unnecessary within a decade was given during the DOJ's search-monopoly remedies trial against Google (US v. Google), the same proceeding that threatens to void the ~$20 billion/year Google-Apple default search payment.
  > Eddy Cue, Apple's senior vice president of services, made the disclosure Wednesday during his testimony in the US Justice Department's lawsuit against Alphabet Inc. The heart of the dispute is the two companies' estimated $20 billion-a-year deal that makes Google the default offering for queries in Apple's browser.
  [[apple-explores-move-to-ai-search-in-browser-amid-google-fallout-bloomberg]]

- OpenAI acquired Jony Ive's AI devices startup io for $6.4 billion in 2025, positioning it to build a post-smartphone consumer AI device.

  [[apple-punted-on-ai-this-year-next-year-will-be-critical]]

- The court found that AI has unquestionably improved general search but has not yet fundamentally altered market dynamics, citing evidence that Bing gained no new distribution or market share despite closing the AI-driven quality gap, and that AI-native entrant Neeva exited the market for lack of distribution.
  > AI has unquestionably improved general search, but it has not yet fundamentally altered market dynamics.
  [[united-states-district-court-2]]

**Proposed resolution:** Interface relocation is a long-horizon tail risk, not a 2-5 year base case. Flag Cue's testimony context (advocacy setting).
