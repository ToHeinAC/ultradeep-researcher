# Interim report: lockin-durability-preference-vs-ai-interface

**Locus question:** Is Apple's preference-based retention durable if AI relocates the primary interface away from the phone — and does any strong-form (phone-level) switching evidence from DMA markets exist to test it?
**Flavor:** dialectical

**Resume note:** this locus was investigated to the point of "all reading done" by a prior instance whose fetches survive in the vault (tag `locus-lockin-durability-preference-vs-ai-interface`) and whose scratch files (`temp/scratch-lockin/set1.json`, `set2.json`, `newsources.json`) I read in full before writing this note. I am not re-fetching anything that instance already secured. I spent 1 of the 2 remaining source-budget units on one genuine gap: a historical analogue for loyalty failing to survive a full interface-paradigm shift (as opposed to the in-vault academic paper's within-paradigm generational shift). That fetch was still in flight when this note was finalized; see the Open questions section for how to fold it in if the orchestrator wants it before drafting.

## What the corpus already said

The contradiction graph already resolved two clusters that this locus is required to reconcile. Cluster 10 (`lockin-structural-vs-removable`, rank 10) concluded: "Retention is structural, but mainly through preference rather than switching cost. Preference-based retention is MORE robust to interoperability mandates than cost-based lock-in would be." Cluster 5 (`ai-interface-relocation`, rank 5) concluded: "Interface relocation is a long-horizon tail risk, not a 2-5 year base case," explicitly discounting Eddy Cue's "no iPhone needed in a decade" testimony because it names a ~10-year horizon and was given "in a proceeding where Apple benefits from portraying Google search as displaceable." Both conclusions are individually defensible on the evidence each cluster examined — but neither cluster tested them against each other, which is exactly the contradiction this locus exists to check: preference is preference *for the current interface*, and if cluster 5's discounted risk is closer than "long-horizon" once you weigh first-party operational evidence (not just Cue's forward-looking testimony), cluster 10's reassurance about interoperability-mandate-robustness stops being the right protection to be reassured about.

The loyalty numbers underlying cluster 10 are strong on their own terms: SellCell (self-reported future intent, 5,000 US respondents) finds 96.4% of iPhone users intend to stay, up from 90.5% in 2019, with 60.8% citing "prefer Apple" (brand preference) against only 17.4% citing ecosystem investment specifically [[iphone-loyalty-hits-964-as-android-users-four-times-more-likely-to-switch-macrum]]. CIRP's revealed-behavior panel (US buyers, what device they owned immediately before purchase) puts iPhone loyalty at 87% in Q1 2026, up from 84% a year earlier, with Android-to-iPhone switching holding in an "11% to 15% band" for years [[iphone-loyalty-rate-climbs-to-87-as-switching-to-android-slows-macrumors]]. But CIRP's own follow-up complicates the "pure preference" reading: about one-quarter of all smartphone buyers switch carriers when buying a new phone, and "loyalty to iOS declines when an iPhone owner moves to a new mobile phone company" — some of the measured 87-89% loyalty rate is a carrier-contract/financing artifact, not unconditional ecosystem stickiness [[apple-loyalty-depends-on-carrier-loyalty]]. Both loyalty datasets are US-only; neither offers an EU/DMA-market breakdown, which is the audited absence this locus was specifically tasked to re-confirm (see below).

On interface relocation, the corpus already carries Eddy Cue's own sworn testimony (May 2025, DOJ v. Google) that "AI technology is moving fast enough that users may not need an iPhone in a decade," given in the same testimony where he disclosed Apple is "actively looking at" revamping Safari toward AI-powered search because of "the potential end of" the ~$20B/year Google search-default deal [[apple-punted-on-ai-this-year-next-year-will-be-critical]] [[apple-explores-move-to-ai-search-in-browser-amid-google-fallout-bloomberg]]. The CEO transition (Cook → Ternus, effective Sept 1, 2026) carried, per the corpus, zero AI-strategy content in any of the primary transition documents — an audited absence that raised, not resolved, this locus's uncertainty score [[john-ternus-is-now-apples-ceo-macrumors]].

## What the new sources say

**The DMA phone-switching-rate absence, re-confirmed a third time.** The European Commission's own May 2026 factsheet on DMA smartphone interoperability — the most authoritative document available on what has actually shipped — describes new cross-OS **device transfer** capability (contacts, calendar, messages, photos, documents, Wi-Fi, passwords, and third-party app data, jointly built by Apple and Google under DMA pressure), explicitly framed by the Commission as designed to "reduce data lock-in and unlock user choice" so "users will be able to choose a new phone... without being locked into an ecosystem due to the complexities associated with transferring their data" [[factsheet-how-the-dma-is-making-smartphones-better-interoperability-and-data-por]]. But as of that May 2026 publication, the tool (AppMigrationKit) is still in **beta/documentation-only** stage — the Commission publishes the capability with zero adoption or usage statistics. I searched the corpus and the width-sweep sources for any EU-market iOS-to-Android switching RATE (Counterpoint, Kantar, GfK, national telecom regulators, Eurostat) and found none; the only rate-level switching data in the entire corpus (SellCell, CIRP) is US-only. This is the third independent confirmation of the same audited absence (once in the width sweep, once by the prior lockin instance, now by me): **no source anywhere in this corpus measures phone-level OS-switching rates in an EU/DMA-covered market, before or after March 2024.** What exists instead is (a) capability-removal evidence (DMA-mandated data portability, eSIM transfer, browser data export) and (b) one qualitative n=1 case study showing that even where DMA-style portability exists on paper, real migration remains costly (next paragraph). Given the deep-inference directive to push once more before accepting an absence, I judge this exhausted: the missing dataset would have to come from a market-research firm's paid report or a national regulator's telecom-switching statistics that are not indexed in any searchable public corpus at the depth available to this run, not from a source I simply failed to find.

**The "exit penalty" case study** [[the-exit-penalty-why-the-dmas-interoperability-rules-fail-the-reality-test-of-pl]] is the single most concrete piece of evidence on *why* the DMA rate data doesn't exist yet and *why* it may stay muted even once collected. Independent researcher Teruki Yagi conducted and logged a real iOS-to-Android migration in Japan (a DMA-adjacent regulatory environment via Google's own global compliance rollout, not the EU itself, but methodologically the closest thing to a controlled n=1 in the corpus) across ~120 accounts/services, 25+ hours, 5 days, and documented 34 distinct friction points. The table is precise about *which* frictions survive nominal DMA-style compliance: Suica (transit-card-in-Apple-Pay) deregistration "requires original iPhone hardware present" even though data portability is nominally satisfied; iMessage history has "no meaningful export or full history transfer" (data + social lock-in, network-effect amplified — this is the cross-platform-messaging cost the DMA's interoperability push has not reached); Apple Keychain's CSV export is "incomplete; 2FA not preserved," forcing manual reconstruction of every credential; Apple Watch functionality is simply unavailable on Android (hardware dependency, unaffected by any data-portability rule). Yagi's framing, grounded in Klemperer (1987) and Farrell & Klemperer (2007) switching-cost theory: the DMA's Article 6 focus on data portability addresses *entry/daily-use* costs but leaves a largely unaddressed "exit penalty" of procedural, hardware, and identity-migration costs. This is a single-author, single-country (Japan, not EU) case study — genuinely low-N, genuinely not a rate statistic — but it is directly on point for the mechanism question the query's Phase-2 framing asks about: it shows that removing formal/legal switching barriers (which the DMA has done) does not automatically remove *practical* ones, which is exactly the gap between "regulation could unwind lock-in" (the UNKNOWN as framed) and "regulation has unwound lock-in" (unobserved, and this case study explains a plausible mechanism for why).

**The academic brand-loyalty paper** [[asymmetries-in-the-effects-of-drivers-of-brand-loyalty-between-early-and]] (Lam & Shankar, Journal of Interactive Marketing, 2014; SEM, n=514, Singapore, ages 18-35; within-subject comparison of loyalty drivers for the SAME brands across an existing technology generation [2.5G] vs. a new one [3G]) is the most rigorous mechanism-level evidence in the corpus for whether preference-based loyalty transfers across a technology-paradigm shift. Two results matter directly:

- **H4 (supported, χ²(1)=7.17, p<.01): perceived value's effect on brand loyalty is markedly weaker for the new generation than the existing one** — unstandardized coefficient .61 (existing tech) vs. .31 (new tech), roughly halved. A consumer's positive value-judgment of a brand formed under the OLD technology paradigm carries over only partially — not fully — into loyalty for that same brand under the NEW paradigm.
- **H5 (supported, χ²(1)=10.13, p<.01): perceived benevolence/trust in the manufacturer becomes a stronger, POSITIVE driver of loyalty for the new generation** (coefficient .18, positive) versus being negative/non-driving for the existing generation (-.18). The authors' own mechanism: "as uncertainty increases in the new generation, benevolence becomes salient in reducing consumers' vulnerability... perceived benevolence becomes an important loyalty driver for the new generation."

The paper's own scope is a 2.5G→3G shift within the same product category (mobile phones), which is a *smaller* paradigm shift than phone-to-AI-interface relocation — it is an analogy, not a direct empirical test of the locus question, and the paper's authors do not discuss AI or interface relocation at all. But the mechanism it isolates is exactly the one this locus needs: **preference tied to specific technology attributes (value judgments about what the current device DOES) does not fully transfer across a paradigm shift and must be partly re-earned, while diffuse trust in the manufacturer/brand is more transferable and becomes MORE important, not less, as uncertainty rises.** Applied to Apple: the 60.8% of SellCell respondents who cite "prefer Apple" (closer to the paper's benevolence/trust construct) are on firmer ground across an interface shift than a hypothetical respondent whose loyalty rested on specific interface-value judgments (closer to the paper's perceived-value construct) — but the paper's own numbers say even the transferable component is not fully preserved (coefficient does not merely persist, it flips sign and changes meaning), and the non-transferable component (perceived value) is cut roughly in half.

**Forrester's DMA/AI-assistant security piece** [[the-eus-digital-markets-act-meets-the-mobile-os-round-two]] is low-relevance for this locus's core question — it is entirely about malware/spyware risk from DMA Article 6(7)'s OS-level-access mandate for third-party AI assistants, with no switching-rate data — but it does confirm one structural fact worth naming: Article 6(7) is the specific legal mechanism that could let a third-party AI assistant (Gemini, ChatGPT, a future OpenAI/io product) obtain **native OS-level access on an iPhone** without requiring the user to switch phones at all. That is a distinct and arguably faster channel for "interface relocation" than full device migration — it would not require Apple to lose a single unit of hardware share, only to lose control of the *interaction layer* running on its own hardware.

**Cross-locus evidence read in full (per this run's directive):** the Google-TAC interim note [[interim-report-google-tac-magnitude-repricing-and-appellate-tail]] documents, from Judge Mehta's own remedies opinion, that "Apple's own SVP Eddy Cue testified under oath... that Safari search-query volume declined 'for the first time in 22 years,' attributed to GenAI chatbot emergence" and that the court's own stated reason for choosing a shorter six-year remedy term over the plaintiffs' proposed ten was its bet that "technological advancement and product differentiation," not "a decade-long judicial decree," will actually discipline Google. This is a **first-party, sworn, court-accepted factual finding about a metric Apple controls and discloses to a court under oath** — categorically stronger evidence than Cue's own forward-looking "no iPhone in a decade" remark, which cluster 5 correctly flagged as advocacy-tinted forecasting. The AI-late-follower interim note [[interim-report-ai-late-follower-chosen-vs-revealed]] documents new CEO Ternus's Sept 9, 2026 keynote framing — "there's no product in the world better designed to be your intelligent personal hub than iPhone" — as the *only* strategic AI-interface statement Apple's leadership has made, delivered after the Gemini deal was signed and explicitly characterized by that investigator as "post-hoc" narrative-setting rather than evidence of a considered origin strategy; when pressure-tested on a July 2026 earnings call about competitive threats from an OpenAI/SpaceX-style AI device, Ternus gave a content-free non-answer ("I guess I would just reiterate what Tim said... we're just really focused on our plans").

## Evidence synthesis

### Position A: Preference is the more durable moat, and DMA-style regulation cannot touch it because it was never priced on switching cost

The strongest version of this case does not need to deny any of the AI evidence — it argues the AI evidence is scoped to a different, longer horizon than the one that matters for a 2-5 year holding view. SellCell's own data shows ecosystem-investment (the switching-cost-coded reason) is a minority motive even among loyal users (17.4%), while general brand preference is the majority motive (60.8%) [[iphone-loyalty-hits-964-as-android-users-four-times-more-likely-to-switch-macrum]]. CIRP's revealed-behavior data corroborates this is not merely stated intent: Android-to-iPhone switching has sat in an 11-15% band "for years," well below the wide-open-market share Apple captured in the iPhone's early expansion years [[iphone-loyalty-rate-climbs-to-87-as-switching-to-android-slows-macrumors]]. The NBER browser-choice-screen study — even on its corrected, Mozilla-affiliated-but-methodologically-real figures (91% higher Firefox usage on iOS, 13% on Android from month 15, not Mozilla's misreported 113%/12%) — found that *where Safari did lose share, the gains went mostly to Chrome, not to challenger browsers*, meaning the DMA's own natural experiment shows brand-driven consolidation among strong incumbents rather than diffuse consumer flight from Apple's defaults [[active-choice-interventions-at-scale-browser-defaults-under-the-digital-markets]]. On this reading, Cue's "no iPhone in a decade" testimony is advocacy in an adversarial proceeding where Apple's stated interest is to make the Google relationship look replaceable — precisely cluster 5's discount — and Ternus's "iPhone as intelligent hub" framing, whatever its origin, is at minimum a coherent counter-strategy: rather than cede the interaction layer, Apple is positioning to make the phone the AI orchestration point, leveraging exactly the preference asset (60.8% "I prefer Apple") that this position claims is brand-general and portable across whatever runs on top of the hardware.

### Position B: Preference is preference FOR an interface, and the strongest first-party evidence says that interface is already leaking usage to AI

The strongest version of this case rests on distinguishing sworn, court-accepted, backward-looking FACT from forward-looking, interested-party FORECAST — a distinction cluster 5 applied correctly to discount Cue's "decade" remark but did not apply to the *other* half of the same testimony. Cue did not merely predict a future risk; he disclosed, under oath, that **Safari search-query volume already declined for the first time in 22 years**, and attributed it to GenAI query migration [[interim-report-google-tac-magnitude-repricing-and-appellate-tail]]. Twenty-two years without a decline, ending now, is not a decade-out forecast — it is a present-tense, already-realized data point about the exact interaction surface (search-via-browser-on-the-phone) this locus is asking about, disclosed by the party with the best information and the least incentive to overstate it (a decline in Safari usage is bad news for Apple's own $20B+/year TAC annuity, which the same testimony was otherwise defending). The academic brand-loyalty paper's mechanism directly predicts what should happen next if this pattern continues: the "perceived value" component of loyalty — tied to what the CURRENT interface does well — should weaken (H4, coefficient roughly halved across a technology-generation shift), while only the more diffuse "prefer Apple" trust component partially carries over, and even that component changes its functional form rather than persisting unchanged (H5, sign flip) [[asymmetries-in-the-effects-of-drivers-of-brand-loyalty-between-early-and]]. Meanwhile, the DMA's Article 6(7) interoperability push for third-party AI assistants — wake words, system-wide access points, screen automation — is a mechanism (documented in the EC's own factsheet) by which a competing AI assistant could seize the *interaction layer* on Apple's own hardware without any phone being sold or switched at all, making "iPhone unit share" the wrong metric to watch for this specific risk [[factsheet-how-the-dma-is-making-smartphones-better-interoperability-and-data-por]]. And the one first-party strategic response the corpus has (Ternus's Sept 9 keynote) is, per the sibling AI-locus investigator's independent read, post-hoc narrative delivered after the underlying execution failure (an 18-month Siri delay chain, a $250M consumer settlement, an AI/ML-chief retirement) was already locked in, and Ternus gave a content-free answer the one time a journalist pressure-tested it on an earnings call [[interim-report-ai-late-follower-chosen-vs-revealed]] — i.e., the "we have a plan to keep the interface on the device" claim is asserted, not yet demonstrated by execution.

## Committed position

The two clusters this locus was built to reconcile are not actually in contradiction once properly scoped, but reconciling them changes what "durable" means. Cluster 10 is right that Apple's loyalty is preference-dominated (60.8% vs. 17.4%) and therefore MORE robust than switching-cost-based lock-in would be **against the specific threat the DMA natural experiment tests: interoperability mandates that lower the cost of choosing a different provider for a given interface (browser choice screens, app sideloading, data portability).** But cluster 5's "long-horizon tail" verdict on AI interface relocation was reached without weighing the strongest available first-party evidence — Cue's sworn disclosure of a realized, present-tense 22-year-record Safari search-volume decline attributed to GenAI migration — which this locus's cross-reads surfaced only after cluster 5 was drafted. That fact reframes the question: it is not "will AI relocate the interface in 10 years," it is "AI-driven query migration is already measurably underway on Apple's own devices, on Apple's own admission, inside the current window." The academic brand-loyalty mechanism (Lam & Shankar) gives a reason to expect this to matter for retention specifically, not just for search revenue: preference tied to what the current interface does (perceived value) does not survive a paradigm shift intact — it is cut roughly in half in the one rigorously-measured analogous case in the corpus — while only the more diffuse brand-trust component partially transfers, and even that component's functional role changes. **The annuity class's durability does depend, in part, on the interface staying materially fixed within the 2-5 year window** — not because Apple's loyalty numbers are fake or about to collapse wholesale (there is no evidence of that; iPhone unit revenue grew 21.7-23.3% YoY every FY2026 quarter per the contradiction graph's own cluster-5 citation), but because the mechanism protecting that loyalty (general brand preference) is demonstrably less protective of the *specific* revenue streams built on top of the current interface (Safari-mediated search, App Store discovery, in-app purchase flows) than it is of the *hardware* purchase decision itself. Regulation is not the mechanism that unwinds this — DMA-style interoperability mandates test and largely fail to unwind PHONE-level lock-in (no EU switching-rate data exists after three independent searches, and the one granular case study available shows real exit penalties surviving nominal compliance) — but AI-driven interface migration is a documented, already-underway, non-regulatory mechanism that erodes the value of the interface itself, and the query's framing (courts/regulators voiding "arrangements") should be read as intersecting this locus only insofar as the Google-TAC annuity and App Store commission streams are interface-dependent revenue lines threatened by a channel (AI query migration) no antitrust remedy addresses.

- **Position:** Apple's brand-preference loyalty is real, measured, and more resistant to DMA-style interoperability regulation than switching-cost-based lock-in would be — but that resistance protects the HARDWARE purchase decision, not the value of everything built on top of the current interface. Interface relocation is not a long-horizon (10-year) tail risk as cluster 5 concluded; it is already underway inside the 2-5 year window via a first-party-admitted, court-accepted, present-tense metric (Safari search-volume decline), and the annuity-like Services revenue tied to that interface (search TAC, App Store discovery-driven commissions) should be modeled as more exposed to AI-driven migration than to any regulatory remedy.
- **Confidence:** Medium. High confidence on the facts (Cue's sworn testimony content, the CIRP/SellCell loyalty numbers, the NBER corrected figures, the exit-penalty case study's documented friction points, the Lam & Shankar coefficients) — these are primary-document- or peer-reviewed-verified. Medium-to-low confidence on the INFERENCE that connects them (that the academic paper's 2.5G→3G mechanism generalizes to a phone→AI-interface shift, and that a single sworn "first decline in 22 years" data point constitutes "already underway" rather than a one-quarter blip) — both are reasoned extrapolations from adjacent evidence, not direct measurements of the phenomenon itself.
- **Boundary conditions:** This position holds for interface-DEPENDENT revenue (search-default economics, App Store/browser-mediated discovery and commissions) specifically; it does NOT extend to hardware unit economics, where the evidence (87-96% US loyalty, accelerating iPhone revenue, no measured EU switching-rate erosion) supports durability essentially unconditionally within the window. It also does not extend past the 2-5 year window into the "no iPhone needed" 10-year scenario cluster 5 discounted — that discount was correct for the device-obsolescence claim; it was simply applied one clause too far, to the search-volume-decline claim that sits in the same testimony but is a different (and already-realized) fact.
- **What would change this position:** (1) A second and third consecutive year of Safari search-volume decline of comparable or greater magnitude — this would convert "already underway" from a single data point to a trend, materially raising confidence. A reversal or flattening would weaken it substantially. (2) Any EU/DMA-market phone-level switching-rate dataset becoming available (Counterpoint, Kantar, a national regulator) — if it showed material iOS-to-Android net switching increases post-DMA, that would independently corroborate the "lock-in is more removable than cluster 10 concluded" reading and strengthen this position further; if it showed continued stasis even years post-DMA, that would strengthen Position A's regulation-proof claim specifically (while leaving the AI-channel argument untouched, since AI relocation is not a regulatory mechanism). (3) Evidence that Apple Intelligence/Siri AI (general release Sept 14, 2026) captures rather than cedes AI-mediated query volume back to Safari/native apps — broad-sample post-launch usage data, not the two early-access reviews the sibling AI locus found, would be the single most decisive near-term test.
- **Evidence weight:** Directly supporting Position B: 1 sworn court-accepted first-party fact (Safari search-volume decline), 1 peer-reviewed SEM study with two significant, on-point coefficients (Lam & Shankar), 1 documented n=1 case study of real exit-penalty frictions surviving DMA compliance, 1 EC primary-source factsheet confirming AI-assistant OS-access is a live, DMA-driven mechanism. Directly supporting Position A: 2 independent loyalty surveys (SellCell, CIRP) with large, consistent, multi-year figures, 1 corrected NBER DiD study showing incumbent-to-incumbent (not incumbent-to-challenger) browser substitution. Genuinely unresolved: no source in either direction supplies EU/DMA phone-level switching-RATE data, which is the single dataset that would let either position be tested directly rather than by analogy and adjacent metrics.

## Open questions

- The historical-analogue fetch (Nokia/BlackBerry loyalty collapse across the feature-phone-to-smartphone paradigm shift) was in flight when this note was finalized under time pressure; if it lands before drafting, it should be folded in as a second, stronger analogue to the Lam & Shankar 2.5G→3G finding — a FULL interface-paradigm shift rather than a within-category generational one — and would likely sharpen (not reverse) the committed position above, since Nokia/BlackBerry's loyalty collapse is the canonical real-world instance of exactly this failure mode.
- No EU/DMA-market phone-level (not browser-level) switching-rate dataset exists in any source found across three independent search passes (width sweep, prior lockin instance, this instance). This should be reported to the orchestrator as a settled audited absence, not re-searched again in Layer 6+.
- Whether Apple Intelligence/Siri AI's Sept 14, 2026 general release reverses, flattens, or extends the Safari search-volume decline is unknowable as of this writing (the note was finalized one day before general release); this is the single most decision-relevant near-term data point and should be flagged for the orchestrator as a "watch item" the report itself cannot resolve.
- The exact scope of the DMA's draft Android AI-assistant interoperability measures (public consultation closed 13 May 2026) — whether Apple's iOS gets an equivalent mandate, and on what timeline — was not fully traced; the EC factsheet covers only the Android-side draft measures in detail.
