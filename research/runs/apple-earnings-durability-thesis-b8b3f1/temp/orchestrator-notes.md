# Orchestrator notes — apple-earnings-durability-thesis-b8b3f1

## Step 2 width sweep — emerging picture

### The binary is less binary than the brief assumed

The brief classified Google TAC as `binary` — probability-weight it, don't
multiply it. Early evidence complicates that:

- Mehta's remedies ruling did **not** ban Google's payments to Apple. It
  barred *exclusive* distribution deals and capped agreement terms at one
  year. DOJ and states have appealed.
- So the exposure is not "payment survives or dies". It is **repricing under
  annual renegotiation with non-exclusivity**. That is closer to a
  short-duration contingent annuity than a coin flip.

This is a genuine correction to the brief's own frame, and exactly what the
falsification test should surface. Worth flagging to the loci analysts: the
durability taxonomy may need a fifth treatment — "repriceable annuity" —
between `contingent` and `binary`.

### Reader prior 2 (payment-rail disruption) is trending toward refuted, but for
### an interesting reason

Stablecoin evidence for 2026: B2B/cross-border is where volume is
(~$226B annually, +733% YoY), while consumer retail settlement remains a pilot.
Card networks are absorbing rather than being displaced — Visa/Mastercard are
building the rails themselves. So crypto rails do not look like a 2-5y threat
to Apple Pay.

BUT App Store commissions *are* eroding — US commission revenue reportedly
-18% YTD, EU steering restrictions ordered removed, Epic injunction forcing
external purchase links. The erosion is real and the reader sensed it. The
*cause* is regulatory, not technological.

That is the sharpest available finding: **the prior is directionally right
about the outcome and wrong about the mechanism.** A report that just says
"no, crypto won't disrupt payments" would be answering the letter of the
prior and missing its substance. The synthesizer must engage both halves.

### Reader prior 1 (late-follower advantage) — genuine tension forming

Against: Siri revamp has slipped repeatedly (spring 2025 → spring 2026 →
iOS 27), which is execution failure, not patient capital allocation. Three
years of delay is not "waiting for inference to commoditise".

For: Apple's capex remains a fraction of hyperscaler AI spend while its
installed base is intact, and inference costs are falling — the option to buy
capability later is real and cheaper each year.

The dialectical question: is Apple's position *chosen* (option value) or
*revealed* (they tried and could not execute)? Those look identical on a
capex line and imply opposite things for a 2-5y view. This is the strongest
candidate for a dialectical locus.

### Replacement cycle — the bear case has hard numbers

Average iPhone replacement age 2.9y (2022) → 3.7-3.84y (2025-26). Share of
buyers replacing a ≤2-year-old device fell 41% (2020) → 29% (2025). That is
a structural lengthening, and it directly attacks the "AI supercycle" thesis.
Cyclical earnings class is deteriorating on measurable evidence.

### Supply chain — diversification is faster than the brief assumed

China share of iPhone assembly 83% → 74% (2025), India 14% → 23% (2025),
projected 28% (2026). The brief called diversification "physically slow"
(load-bearing barrier). The data says it is happening at ~9pp/year. Another
place where evidence may overturn a Phase 1 assumption — good.

### Where I expect the thesis to land

The falsification test is the pivot. If the durability-weighted blended
multiple ≈ the headline multiple, the whole frame is decoration and the report
must say so. My prior is that it will NOT be equal, for a specific reason:
the market prices Apple's Services as a single high-multiple annuity, but
Services contains at least three durability classes with very different
risk — subscriptions (genuine annuity), App Store commissions (contingent,
already eroding in reported numbers), and the Google fee (repriceable).
Consensus applies one multiple to all three. That is the mispricing the frame
would expose, if it exists.

The strongest disconfirming possibility: the contingent + repriceable classes
may simply be too small a share of total earnings to move a blended multiple
by more than a point or two. Apple's earnings are dominated by iPhone gross
profit. If TAC plus App Store commissions together are, say, 15-20% of
operating income, then even aggressive haircuts move the blended multiple
modestly — and the honest conclusion is that the frame is analytically
correct but not decision-relevant. The report must be willing to reach that
conclusion. Sizing those two lines is therefore the highest-stakes number in
the whole run.

### A taxonomy problem the loci analysts should inherit

The brief's four classes may not survive contact with the evidence:

- **Binary is probably wrong for TAC.** Mehta permitted payments, barred
  exclusivity, capped terms at one year. That is a repriceable annuity under
  annual renegotiation, not a coin flip. Different valuation treatment
  entirely: haircut the rate, do not probability-weight the existence.
- **Contingent is probably right for App Store commissions, and is already
  realising.** Not a future risk — reported US commission revenue is
  reportedly down double digits and Apple itself has cited "App Store
  business-model changes" as a drag on Services growth. If confirmed from a
  primary source, that converts the class from speculative to measured.
- **Annuity-like needs a stickiness test it has not been given.** Subscription
  revenue is only an annuity if the installed base is sticky. The DMA
  evidence tests a weak form of that (browser switching, nearly costless);
  the strong form (phone switching, very costly) may have no natural
  experiment at all. If so, that absence is itself a finding and the annuity
  class carries more uncertainty than the market prices.

The honest synthesis: Apple made a capital-STRUCTURE choice (opex/lease over
capex/own), not a capital-RESTRAINT choice. That is a genuinely different
claim from the reader's, and it is defensible from primary filings. It also
implies the option-value argument is weaker than the reader thinks — you do
not need to preserve optionality if you are already spending.

Liability attaches to the ISA's **de facto exclusivity, not the payment
mechanism**. The court itself cites the pre-2005 non-exclusive default
arrangement as a legally viable, less-restrictive counterfactual. So the

appeal" tail.

Analyst proposes a three-state model: status quo / non-exclusive-but-paid
