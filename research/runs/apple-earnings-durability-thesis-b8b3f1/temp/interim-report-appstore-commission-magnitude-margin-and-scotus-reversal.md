## What the corpus already said

The width sweep had converged on a single commercial estimator for commission revenue — Appfigures' ~$10.1B US / ~$27.4B global figure for 2024 — and an unevidenced ~90% margin assumption for the orchestrator's falsification test. The corpus's own contradiction graph (`temp/contradiction-graph.json`, cluster 6, `appstore-erosion-mechanism`) had already flagged the central tension this locus is assigned to adjudicate: "Is App Store commission revenue genuinely eroding under regulation, or have structural remedies had little practical effect?" Side A (erosion is real and already in reported numbers) cited the zero-commission US link-out regime since April 2025, the Appfigures -18% YTD figure, and CFO Kevan Parekh's earnings-call attribution. Side B (remedies have barely dented the model) cited EU alternative-marketplace adoption staying below 3% since March 2024 despite being legally permitted, and the fact that only the $149B digital-goods slice of Apple's self-reported $1.4T "ecosystem" billings is commissionable at all. Cluster 7 (`appstore-fee-relabeling`) already established the sequential pattern: Apple relabels a fee (27% US link-out commission, 5% EU Core Technology Commission) → courts/regulators strike it down as evasion → Apple is worse off than before it tried. No prior note in the corpus had read the SCOTUS docket closely enough to state the actual question presented, nor had any note traced the 78% figure back to its evidentiary source rather than repeating the 9to5Mac headline.

## What the new sources say

*(These sources were already in the vault from the prior investigator's fetch pass; this section reflects my own full-text reading of them.)*

**[[full-findings-of-the-court-in-epic-games-v-apple-pdf-ios-computing]]** — Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers's 2021 Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law. This is the actual court order, not a summary. It states Apple "enjoys considerable market share of over 55% and extraordinarily high profit margins" but — critically — **does not adopt or cite the 78% (or 79.6%) figure anywhere in the order's text.** I searched the full body for "78%," "profit margin" values, "Barnes," and "operating income" — none of the specific percentages appear. What the order does say: "gaming apps account for approximately 70% of all App Store revenues," generated by "less than 10% of all App Store consumers," and Apple's standard IAP split is "remits 70% to the developers, and keeps a 30% commission," a rate that "has largely remained unchanged since inception." The court found this rate produces "excessive operating margins" but declined to certify a specific number as a finding of fact.

**[[apple-runs-app-store-with-78-profit-margin-according-to-court-testimony-9to5mac]]** — traces the 78% figure to its actual source: trial testimony from **Ned Barnes, an Epic Games damages expert witness**, not a court finding and not Apple's own disclosure. Direct quote: "This figure comes from a court testimony from an Epic Games expert witness Ned Barnes. The profit margin rose from 75% in 2018 to 78% in 2019, according to the calculations from the witness." Crucially: "As part of the discovery process, Barnes said that an Apple employee told him that the numbers from Apple's internal documents don't show the full picture. Barnes said he then made additional calculations which resulted in higher margin estimates of 79.6% for both 2018 and 2019." So there are two Barnes numbers — 75%/78% from Apple's own internal documents as Barnes read them, and a Barnes-adjusted 79.6% after he decided Apple's documents understated true margin. Apple disputed the figures at trial ("Apple will naturally argue that the figures are inaccurate or inflated"). This is one side's expert's litigation-position number, never adopted by the court as fact, contested by the other party, sourced from confidential internal Apple financial documents whose methodology is not public.

**[[epic-games-wins-injunction-against-apple-pdf-legal-remedy-injunction]]** — the actual April 30, 2025 contempt order (via Scribd), which supplies the term used in the underlying 2021 finding of fact: the court found the original 30% commission "allowed it to reap **supracompetitive operating margins**" — confirming the metric class is operating margin, not gross margin, and not Apple's Services-segment gross margin as reported in the 10-K. The order also documents Apple's course of conduct after the original injunction: Apple imposed a new 27% commission (where it had "previously charged nothing") on off-app purchases plus a 7-day extension window and "scare screens," which the court found was "in direct defiance" of the injunction and designed to "maintain its anticompetitive revenue stream... worth billions." The court found Apple acted with "express intent to create new anticompetitive barriers" and that a VP of Finance's trial testimony diverged from what "contemporaneous business documents reveal."

**[[aapl-20260627]]** (Apple's own Q3 FY2026 10-Q, SEC EDGAR, primary document, filed ~July 2026) — the single most load-bearing source for the SCOTUS-consequence question, because it is Apple's own sworn legal-proceedings disclosure, current as of the query date. Direct quote: "On April 30, 2025, the California District Court found the Company to be in violation of the 2021 Injunction and enjoined the Company from imposing any commission or any fee on purchases that consumers make outside an app... The Ninth Circuit Court also held that the Company can charge some commission on link-out purchases, and remanded to the California District Court to further amend or modify the 2025 Injunction... On May 21, 2026, the Company filed a petition seeking Supreme Court review... On June 30, 2026, the Supreme Court granted the Company's petition to review the applicable legal standard for civil contempt. **The Company is seeking a stay of the California District Court proceedings pending the Supreme Court's decision** on the question under review." The risk-factor section separately states that if third-party developers route around Apple's IAP, "the Company may earn a lower commission on such sales, or may not earn a commission at all, which can materially adversely affect the Company's revenue, gross margin, results of operations, financial condition and stock price" — Apple's own words, confirming management sees this as a gross-margin-level risk, not just a Services-line risk.

**[[apple-inc-v-epic-games-inc-25-1311-scotusblog]]** (SCOTUSblog docket, primary record) gives the exact Question Presented and the timeline that resolves the stay question the 10-Q leaves open: "Application (26A194) for a stay, submitted to Justice Kagan" on Aug 12, 2026; a 24-hour **administrative** stay was granted the same day purely to allow time to consider the application; then on **Aug 13, 2026, the stay application was denied**. This means Apple's request to freeze the district-court remand proceeding (the one that will set what commission, if any, Apple may charge going forward) while SCOTUS decides the contempt-standard question **failed**. The remand proceeding is not paused. Docket also confirms: cert petition filed May 21, 2026; granted June 30, 2026, "limited to Question 1 presented by the petition"; opening brief on the merits due September 14, 2026 (extended from an earlier date); Epic's brief due November 13, 2026 — meaning oral argument almost certainly falls in the October 2026 term's spring sitting (Feb–Apr 2027) at the earliest, with a decision plausible by June 2027, arguably later given typical scheduling after a fall-2026 argument slot fills.

**Question 1, exact text** (per SCOTUSblog): "Whether a court may hold a party in civil contempt based on a violation of an injunction's 'spirit' where the injunction is silent as to the conduct upon which contempt is based, or, instead, whether a court must ground a finding of civil contempt on the violation of an order that clearly and unambiguously proscribes the precise conduct at issue." This is confirmed verbatim in **[[us-supreme-court-will-consider-challenge-to-contempt-order-in-federal-antitrust]]** (Crowell & Moring) and **[[high-court-grants-cert-in-apples-challenge-to-ninth-circuit-contempt-ruling-in-a]]** (IPWatchdog). All three law-firm/legal-press sources agree: the Court explicitly **declined** to grant Apple's second question — whether the Ninth Circuit created an improper "antitrust exception" to *Trump v. CASA* (which would have questioned the injunction's scope reaching non-party developers). Per **[[the-supreme-court-took-apples-epic-appeal-but-only-on-one-narrow-question]]** (MacObserver): "That phrase excludes Apple's App Store commission and steering rules from review, even though the grant is widely treated as the Court agreeing to revisit the App Store ruling itself... The commission and steering questions themselves stayed with the Ninth Circuit's ruling and the pending remand, both untouched by the cert grant."

**[[epic-games-ninth-circuit-win-affirming-civil-contempt-finding-cravath-swaine-moo]]** (Cravath, Epic's counsel — read for its factual recitation, not its advocacy framing) confirms the Ninth Circuit (Dec 11, 2025) "unanimously affirmed" the contempt finding itself, affirmed most sanctions, but "modif[ied] or revers[ed] in part certain other sanctions," and held "although Apple should be allowed to charge some fees on linked transactions, those should be limited **and tied to the costs of any services Apple provides in facilitating steering links**" — i.e., a cost-plus standard, not a percentage-of-transaction-value standard. The Ninth Circuit separately affirmed denial of Apple's request to vacate/modify the underlying permanent injunction and denied reassignment to a different judge on remand.

**[[support-for-this-study-was-provided-by-apple]]** (Apple's Analysis Group-commissioned "Global App Store Ecosystem" study, 2025 edition) supplies the denominator context for the 90%-escapes-commission framing the reader-prior challenges the report to test: total App Store-facilitated billings $1.437T globally; "Apple collected no commission on more than 90%" of that; the commissionable slice is "digital goods and services" — direct quote: "Commission: Less than 10% / No commission: More than 90%." **[[how-apple-makes-billions-from-the-app-storeand-why-90-of-sales-now-escape-its-co]]** (IBTimes) decomposes this Apple-commissioned number: physical goods/services facilitated = $1.1T (Uber, Amazon, grocery, travel — zero commission by design, was never Apple's to tax); digital goods/services (games, streaming, subscriptions, enterprise software) = "$149B in 2025... up from $131B... a year before," taxed "15% to 30%." The same article restates Appfigures' commission-revenue estimates: "$10.1B" US 2024 (from "$4.76B" in 2020 — more than doubling in four years) and "$27.39B" global 2024.

**[[apples-us-app-store-commission-revenue-down-18-this-year-macrumors]]** and **[[apple-makes-rare-admission-of-regulatory-hit-as-app-store-commission-model-erode]]** (BigGo, citing the same underlying Appfigures/Sensor Tower data) supply the 2026 erosion trajectory: US commission revenue "fallen 18 percent since the start of 2026" (Appfigures); US App Store consumer spending "down 6 percent" in Q2 2026 "compared to 9 percent growth a year earlier" (Sensor Tower); globally, App Store consumer spending growth decelerated from "13% growth a year earlier" to "just 3% year-over-year in the first half" of 2026 — a global deceleration far too large to be explained by the US-only injunction alone, implicating Brazil, Japan, and EU DMA pressure simultaneously. Apple's 10-Q itself now states (per BigGo, corroborated by the 10-Q's own risk-factor language quoted above) that commission on alternative-payment transactions "may be entirely ineligible for commission" — language BigGo calls a "rare admission."

**[[one-last-time-this-is-tim-final-transcript-of-apples-q3-2026-earnings-call-six-c]]** (Six Colors full transcript, primary source) — CFO Kevan Parekh, verbatim: "We also had some factors that impacted the performance of the App Store. We did see some headwinds in mobile gaming, and then keep in mind, we also made some changes to the App Store business model in certain countries, and in the U.S., we do continue to operate under a court ruling impacting the link-out transactions. But we're pleased the Supreme Court will hear our appeal. Despite this, the App Store did set a June quarter revenue record." Services growth decelerated from "16 percent" (fiscal Q2 2026) to "12%" (fiscal Q3 2026, $30.7B, missing consensus of ~$31.4B per MacRumors), with guidance implying "below 10% year-over-year" for fiscal Q4. Parekh's attribution mixes three causes in one breath — mobile-gaming softness (not litigation-related), "App Store business model" changes in unnamed other countries (likely EU/DMA-driven, not litigation-related), and the US court ruling (litigation-related) — without apportioning the deceleration among them. This is Apple choosing not to quantify the litigation-specific drag even while acknowledging it exists.

## Evidence synthesis

**On revenue magnitude:** every dollar figure for App Store commission revenue in this corpus — US and global — traces to two commercial analytics vendors, Appfigures and Sensor Tower, not to any court filing, SEC disclosure, or party admission. Apple has never disclosed App Store commission revenue as a line item; it is buried inside the $109.2B FY2025 Services net-sales figure (10-K, `research/runs/.../scratch-appstore/10k.json`) alongside Apple Music, iCloud, AppleCare, advertising, and Apple Pay. The court record (2021 findings of fact) supplies one hard, judicially-found anchor that no vendor estimate can contradict: gaming apps generate "approximately 70% of all App Store revenues," from under 10% of App Store consumers, on the standard 70/30 split. Apple's own commissioned study (2025) supplies a second anchor: digital-goods-and-services billings subject to any commission were $131B (2024) and $149B (2025) globally, taxed at 15-30% depending on developer tier. Cross-checking Appfigures' $27.39B global 2024 commission-revenue estimate against Apple's own $131B 2024 digital-billings figure implies a blended effective take rate of ~20.9% — a plausible number given the Small Business Program's 15% tier and the second-year-subscription 15% rate coexist with the headline 30% rate, and this internal consistency is the strongest evidence that the Appfigures estimate is in the right order of magnitude, even though its exact precision cannot be verified.

**On margin:** this is where the corpus's ~90% assumption is not just unevidenced but wrong in kind. The 78%/79.6% figures are (a) an advocate expert witness's (Ned Barnes, retained by Epic) litigation calculation, (b) for 2018-2019 specifically, seven years stale relative to the query's fiscal-year requirement, (c) explicitly disputed by Apple at trial, (d) never adopted by the court as a finding of fact — the court's own language is qualitative ("extraordinarily high," "excessive," "supracompetitive") and deliberately avoids certifying a number, and (e) an **operating margin**, not a gross margin — the contempt order's own language calls it "supracompetitive operating margins." The falsification test's methodology needs a gross margin (to compare against Apple's blended or Products gross margin), and an operating margin is definitionally lower than the corresponding gross margin for the same business because it nets out allocated R&D, App Review staffing, trust-and-safety, marketing, and corporate overhead. There is no disclosed, non-adversarial bridge from Barnes's 78% App-Store-specific operating margin to a gross margin figure. The best available proxy for a *gross* margin is Apple's disclosed Services segment gross margin — 70.8% (FY2023), 73.9% (FY2024), 75.4% (FY2025), computed directly from the 10-K's Services gross margin dollars ($60,345M / $71,050M / $82,314M) over Services net sales ($85,200M / $96,169M / $109,158M). Because App Store commission has near-zero marginal cost (server/App Review costs) relative to lower-margin Services lines bundled into the same segment (AppleCare involves physical repair logistics; hardware-embedded service amortization carries allocated cost), the App-Store-specific gross margin is very likely *above* the 70.8-75.4% Services blend — which puts Barnes's 75-79.6% *operating*-margin figures in a range that is directionally plausible as a *gross*-margin figure too, but this is my own inference bridging two different metrics from two different sources, not something any single source states. I flag this explicitly as inference, not evidence.

**On the SCOTUS mechanics:** the corpus's dialectical tension resolves cleanly once the actual Question Presented is read against Apple's own 10-Q. Side A (near-term consumer prior: crypto/AI rails threaten the payments franchise) and the loci-analyst's implicit hope that SCOTUS review might reverse the erosion both overstate what is actually before the Court. SCOTUS granted cert on ONE question only — the *procedural standard* for civil contempt (textual "four corners" vs. Ninth Circuit's "spirit" doctrine) — and explicitly declined the second question that would have reached the injunction's scope. The commission and steering *merits* are not before the Court at all; they remain with the Ninth Circuit's already-final ruling and the pending district-court remand to set "what commission, if any" Apple may charge, under a cost-plus standard the Ninth Circuit already announced. Apple tried to freeze that remand pending the SCOTUS decision and **failed** — Justice Kagan denied the stay application on August 13, 2026. That single fact is the most important thing this locus adds to the corpus: whatever SCOTUS decides on Question 1, the district court is currently free to proceed to set a real-world commission rate for US link-out purchases, and that rate-setting proceeding is happening in parallel with, not blocked by, the Supreme Court's review.

### Position A: SCOTUS could meaningfully reverse the erosion

The strongest version of this case: if the Court adopts the stricter "four corners" standard, the April 2025 contempt finding itself is vulnerable, because it explicitly rested in part on the injunction's "spirit" (the 2021 injunction's text only barred Apple from *prohibiting* buttons/links, not from charging a commission on transactions made through them — Apple's 27% fee arguably didn't violate the literal text). A reversal on the contempt standard could vacate downstream sanctions built on that finding — the discovery sanctions and the criminal referral of an Apple employee, and arguably reopen the question of whether the specific "zero commission" remedy was properly grounded (as opposed to some form of Apple-set fee). Apple's reply brief argues exactly this: the Ninth Circuit's atextual standard "directly conflicts with the text-based standard applied in other circuits," and a coalition of tech trade associations (CCIA, Chamber of Progress, NetChoice, SIIA) filed amicus support arguing the orders imposed "platform-wide changes affecting all developers rather than relief tailored to Epic's specific injury" and implicated Rule 65's specificity requirement. If Apple wins, some restoration of pricing leverage over the *contempt-specific* remedy (the total ban on any fee) is plausible.

### Position B: even a full Apple win is a procedural reprieve, not a commission restoration

The steel-manned case against reversal mattering commercially: (1) the Ninth Circuit's holding that Apple "should be allowed to charge some fees on linked transactions" but only if "limited and tied to the costs of any services Apple provides in facilitating steering links" is a **merits holding on the underlying injunction**, not part of Question 1, and survives regardless of the contempt-standard outcome — meaning a cost-plus (not percentage-of-GMV) ceiling on any US link-out fee is already locked in independent of SCOTUS. (2) Even if SCOTUS narrows the contempt standard prospectively, courts routinely respond to a "no fair notice" reversal by simply re-issuing an explicit, textually unambiguous order — which is exactly what the district court is positioned to do on remand, a remand SCOTUS's own denial of Apple's stay request (Aug 13, 2026) confirms is proceeding right now. A "win" on Question 1 does not restore the 27% rate; the 27% rate was never blessed by any court and remains foreclosed by the merits ruling that stands independent of the contempt-standard question. (3) The district court has already found, as fact, that Apple's VP of Finance's testimony diverged from Apple's own contemporaneous documents and that Apple acted with "express intent" to evade the injunction — a credibility finding that will make any future district-court proceeding (post-SCOTUS, on remand, or in a fresh enforcement motion) far less deferential to Apple's compliance representations. Epic's brief in opposition argues persuasively that there is no real circuit split (the Ninth Circuit found a *textual* violation too — "prohibitive effect" on steering — not solely a spirit violation), meaning even the doctrinal premise for Apple's appeal may not survive scrutiny on the merits.

Position B has the better evidence: it is grounded in Apple's own sworn 10-Q characterization of what is and is not before the Court, in the SCOTUS docket's own narrow issue statement, and in the fact — not spin — that Apple's own stay request was denied by the Court itself just five weeks before this report's writing date. Position A's strongest point (that the contempt sanctions are legally vulnerable) is real but goes to *sanctions*, not to the *commission rate*, which is governed by a separate, already-final merits ruling.

## Committed position

The evidence base for App Store commission economics is genuinely thin — a single pair of commercial estimators for revenue, a single disputed expert-witness figure for margin (misdescribed in the width sweep as a settled 90% assumption, and misdescribed even in its own press coverage as a straightforward "profit margin" when it is actually a contested operating margin from 2018-2019 litigation testimony) — but the SCOTUS mechanics are not thin at all: they are laid out precisely in the docket, in Apple's own 10-Q, and in three independent secondary legal-press accounts that all agree on the narrow scope of Question 1. On that basis I commit to: (1) global App Store commission revenue for the most recent full fiscal year is defensibly bounded at **$22-31B**, with $27.4B (Appfigures 2024, unadjusted) as the labelled upper-commercial-estimator bound, and a lower bound built from applying that estimate's implied ~21% blended take rate to Apple's own $149B 2025 digital-billings figure and then discounting 10-20% for observed 2025-2026 erosion; US commission revenue is bounded at **$8-10B** for the same reason (Appfigures $10.1B 2024 as upper bound, -18% YTD 2026 run-rate implying ~$8.3B as the erosion-adjusted lower bound), with the caveat that these are vendor estimates, not disclosed or court-certified figures. (2) The correct margin figure to carry into any durability/valuation model is Apple's disclosed **Services segment gross margin — 75.4% (FY2025), up from 73.9% (FY2024) and 70.8% (FY2023)** — used as an evidenced floor, with the Barnes 78-79.6% figures cited only as a labelled, contested, stale (2018-19), operating-margin (not gross-margin) upper reference from adversarial litigation testimony that the court itself declined to adopt as fact. Do NOT use ~90% for anything; no source in this corpus supports it at any level of the analysis. (3) SCOTUS's grant is narrow and procedural: even a full Apple win on Question 1 restores, at most, some previously-imposed contempt *sanctions*; it does not revive the 27% commission rate, does not disturb the Ninth Circuit's separate, final merits holding that any US link-out fee must be cost-based rather than percentage-based, and does not pause the district-court remand that is setting that fee right now (Apple's own stay request was denied August 13, 2026). (4) Commission erosion in the US link-out channel is **structurally durable over a 2-5 year holding horizon** — not because any single ruling forecloses Apple's options, but because the pattern across three cycles (2021 injunction → 27% relabeled fee → found in contempt; EU DMA → 5% Core Technology Fee → new preliminary EC findings against it) shows every relabeling attempt has been met with an adverse finding, and a district court that has already found Apple's own executives testified inconsistently with contemporaneous internal documents has essentially zero remaining credibility runway to try a fourth iteration of the same maneuver. The offsetting force — genuinely low real-world uptake of alternative payment/marketplace options even where legally permitted (EU adoption <3% since March 2024, per the corpus's own contradiction graph) — bounds the SPEED of erosion but does not reverse its DIRECTION; Sensor Tower's -6% US Q2 spend and Appfigures' -18% YTD US commission figures show the erosion is already showing up in realized numbers, not merely in theoretical legal exposure.

- **Position:** Commission revenue and margin are real but small relative to Apple's total profit pool (global commission revenue ~$22-31B against ~$110B of Services gross margin dollars and ~$425B of total FY2025 revenue), and the 2-5 year risk is not that the whole $27B disappears but that its per-dollar durability is now measurably worse than the market's 30+ P/E implicitly assumes, because every legal avenue Apple has tried to preserve the percentage-of-GMV commission structure (link-out fee, EU CTF) has been found unlawful or contemptuous, and the one Supreme Court review now underway cannot restore that structure even in Apple's best-case outcome.
- **Confidence:** medium. High confidence on the SCOTUS-scope claims (multiple independent primary/near-primary sources agree verbatim on Question 1's text and the narrow grant); medium confidence on the revenue range (built on two commercial estimators cross-checked against one Apple-commissioned billings figure, no independent audit); low-to-medium confidence on the margin bridge from Barnes's operating margin to a gross-margin equivalent (this is my inference, not a sourced number).
- **Boundary conditions:** the durability conclusion applies specifically to the US link-out commission channel and, by extension, the EU anti-steering/CTF channel, where courts and regulators have already ruled. It does NOT extend with the same confidence to non-gaming digital categories Apple has not yet been forced to open (e.g., most subscription IAP still flows through Apple's own checkout because consumer switching friction remains high — EU's <3% alternative-marketplace adoption is the load-bearing data point here), nor to markets without an active enforcement action (most of Asia ex-Japan, Latin America ex-Brazil).
- **What would change this position:** (a) SCOTUS ruling for Apple on Question 1 AND the district court, on remand, setting a link-out fee close to Apple's original 27% rather than a cost-based fee close to zero — this combination would falsify the "structurally durable erosion" claim; (b) a disclosed Apple financial statement (not a vendor estimate) showing App Store commission revenue growing, not shrinking, in the US in 2026 after the injunction took full effect — this would falsify the revenue-erosion claim; (c) discovery of a non-litigation, non-adversarial source (e.g., an Apple internal document produced in a different proceeding, or a DOJ/FTC economic filing) giving a gross margin figure for App Store specifically — this would replace my inferred gross-margin bridge with real evidence and could move the number materially in either direction.
- **Evidence weight:** 2 primary court documents (2021 findings of fact; April 2025 contempt order) + 1 primary SEC filing (Q3 FY2026 10-Q) + 1 primary docket record (SCOTUSblog) support the SCOTUS-scope and margin-definition claims directly. 3 independent legal-press secondary sources (Crowell, Cravath, IPWatchdog, MacObserver) corroborate the Question 1 text without contradiction. 2 commercial-analytics-sourced secondary articles (MacRumors, BigGo) support the revenue-erosion trend, both ultimately citing the same two vendors (Appfigures, Sensor Tower) — this is *not* independent triangulation, it is one data source cited twice. 1 Apple-commissioned study (Analysis Group) supports the billings decomposition but has an obvious motivated-framing incentive (the "90% escapes commission" headline), which the IBTimes source explicitly deconstructs. No source in the corpus provides an independent, non-adversarial, non-vendor gross margin figure for App Store specifically — this is the single biggest evidentiary gap.

## Open questions

- No source gives a disclosed or court-certified App Store commission GROSS margin figure for any year; the 75.4% Services-segment blend is the best available floor, but a true App Store-only number remains synthetic.
- Attribution of the 2026 Services deceleration (16%→12%→guided <10%) between (a) the US litigation, (b) EU/other-country "App Store business model changes," and (c) mobile-gaming softness is not quantified anywhere, including by Apple itself — Parekh named all three causes in one sentence without apportioning them.
- No source states what commission rate the district court is likely to set on remand under the Ninth Circuit's "cost of services" standard — this is the single number most decision-relevant to the 2-5 year holding view and it does not yet exist because the remand proceeding, though unstayed, has not yet produced a ruling as of this report's writing (Sept 2026).
- Oral argument date for Apple Inc. v. Epic Games (25-1311) is not yet scheduled per the docket as read; my estimate of a spring 2027 argument / June 2027 decision is inferred from the September 2026 opening-brief and November 2026 response-brief deadlines, not stated by any source.
