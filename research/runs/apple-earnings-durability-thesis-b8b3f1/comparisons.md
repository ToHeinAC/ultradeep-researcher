## Tension 1: The court is not the main threat to "court-contingent" revenue — the interface is

- **TAC** commits: TAC is a repriceable annuity; the appellate legal process is a slow, currently low-probability tail (payment ban before 2031 ≈15–20%; the current remedy continuing ≈45–55%), while "GenAI-driven demand migration [is] the faster-moving, higher-probability risk the court's own reasoning treats as dominant."
- **Lock-in** commits: brand-preference loyalty protects the HARDWARE purchase decision, not interface-dependent revenue; AI interface relocation is "already underway inside the 2–5 year window" — overturning contradiction-graph cluster 5's "long-horizon tail" verdict — for search TAC and App Store discovery-driven commissions specifically.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** CONVERGENCE FROM INDEPENDENT PATHS. The TAC investigator reached this conclusion from the court record (the remedies opinion's own reasoning); the lock-in investigator reached it from brand-loyalty research (Lam & Shankar) and first-party testimony. Both anchor on the same fact — Eddy Cue's sworn, court-accepted statement that Safari search-query volume declined for the first time in 22 years, attributed to generative AI — but arrive through different evidence chains. Convergence from independent directions is itself a high-weight finding. It reframes the brief's premise: the query asks which earnings a court or regulator can void, yet the dominant threat to the most exposed of those earnings is a NON-legal mechanism that no antitrust remedy addresses. The same dollars face two independent threats (legal repricing and AI query migration), and they compound rather than overlap.
- **How the draft should engage this:** The binary-events section (heading 4) and the lock-in section (heading 7) must both state that court exposure is the *visible* risk and AI query migration the *faster* one, and the verdict (heading 8) must reframe the report's central question accordingly — contingent on courts AND on the interface, with the interface risk the less legally visible and the more imminent.
- **Calibration:** Both medium confidence. BOTH investigators independently name the same "what would change my mind" condition: further Safari/Siri query-migration data beyond the single "first decline in 22 years" data point (TAC: "a materially faster-than-expected Safari/Siri query migration… would shift the dominant risk channel from legal to demand-side"; Lock-in: "a second and third consecutive year of Safari search-volume decline"). This is a GENUINE OPEN QUESTION — flag it explicitly as the single most important leading indicator for a holder. It rests on one data point; the draft must say so and must not present "already underway" as a trend.

## Tension 2: The brief's taxonomy is inverted — its "binary" stream is the legally durable one; its "contingent" stream is the one actually eroding

- **TAC** commits: NOT binary. The payment-ban question has already resolved in Apple's favour; the operative regime is fixed for six years; price compression is "gradual single-digit-percentage-point annual rate erosion against a growing base, not a step-change loss"; no rival bidder can replace it. Current value $23–29B, plausibly $27–33B by 2031 in the base case.
- **App Store** commits: commission erosion in the US link-out channel is "structurally durable over a 2–5 year holding horizon"; every relabeling attempt (27% US link-out fee, 5% EU Core Technology Fee) has been struck down or attacked; SCOTUS's grant (No. 25-1311) is narrow and procedural, and even a full Apple win cannot restore the 27% rate; the remand setting a cost-based fee proceeds unstayed after Justice Kagan denied Apple's stay on Aug 13 2026. Revenue $22–31B global; erosion already visible in realized numbers.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** COMPLICATION OF THE BRIEF'S OWN FRAME. The brief classified TAC as *binary* (probability-weight it) and App Store commissions as *contingent* (discount for regulatory attack). The evidence inverts their legal risk: TAC is legally MORE durable than assumed (its existence is settled; only its rate drifts down), while commissions are legally LESS durable (erosion is structural and already realised). Yet Tension 1 cuts the other way for TAC — its dominant risk is demand-side. So the two court-exposed streams have OPPOSITE risk vectors: TAC is legally stable but demand-fragile; commissions are legally eroding but demand-sticky (EU alternative-marketplace adoption <3%; in-app purchase friction remains high). A single blended haircut across both — which the brief's frame and the orchestrator's first falsification pass both used — overstates TAC's legal risk and understates commission erosion.
- **How the draft should engage this:** The earnings decomposition (heading 1) and the falsification test (heading 2) must apply DIFFERENT treatments per stream — a modest legal haircut plus an explicit demand-migration scenario for TAC; a larger, realised-erosion haircut for commissions — and must state plainly that the brief's original binary/contingent labels are revised by the evidence, and why.
- **Calibration:** TAC medium (high on the exclusivity-not-payment liability theory and timeline; low-to-medium on the dollar ranges and probability weights, which are constructed estimates). App Store medium (high on SCOTUS scope; medium on revenue; low-to-medium on the margin bridge). Weight the LEGAL conclusions heavily (both rest on primary court documents) and the DOLLAR ranges lightly (both rest on modeled bands). The falsification result survives this weighting: contingent plus repriceable gross profit is 19.7–28.3% of total and 46.7–67.2% of Services across the full low-to-high range.

## Tension 3: The Services cushion that must absorb hardware normalisation is itself eroding — and FY2026 hardware strength is cost pass-through

- **Hardware** commits: FY2026 iPhone strength is overwhelmingly ASP/mix, not units (IDC shipments +4.9% vs iPhone revenue +23.3%), driven by an industry-wide exogenous memory-cost shock rather than an AI upgrade pull; the replacement cycle continues to lengthen; Services' 12–16% growth can hold total revenue flat through an iPhone-X-scale hangover (Products −4% to −6%) but not an iPhone-6-scale one (−8% or worse), and cannot deliver +5% total growth if hardware contracts.
- **App Store** commits: US commission erosion is structural and already visible; CFO Parekh orally attributed part of Services' deceleration (16% → 12%) to App Store business-model changes and the US link-out ruling.
- **Lock-in** commits: preference loyalty protects hardware purchases — so unit demand is durable, but the interface-dependent Services revenue built on top of it is exposed.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** COMPOUNDING COMPLICATION. The hardware locus treats Services as the shock absorber for a hardware mean-reversion. The App Store locus shows part of that absorber is itself being eroded by courts. The margin evidence (contradiction cluster 9: ex-refund gross margin 48.0% → 46.5% guided, "more than 100%" explained by memory costs) shows the SAME memory shock that inflates hardware revenue is compressing margin. So FY2026's headline strength is partly cost pass-through (lower-quality growth under the durability frame), while the cushion meant to protect against its reversal is weakening at the same time. No single locus sees this: it only appears when hardware, App Store and margin evidence are read together. Mitigant: each supercycle hangover has been milder than the last (iPhone 6 −12.8%/−14.6% > iPhone X −4.5%/−5.1% > iPhone 12 positive), plausibly because Services mix has been cushioning for a decade.
- **How the draft should engage this:** The required-services-CAGR analysis must be presented as a DYNAMIC question — the cushion's size is not fixed at 12–16% if part of Services is structurally eroding — and the verdict must name the timing risk: hardware normalisation (FQ4 guide already 9–11% vs 16–17%) arriving while the contingent part of Services erodes. The draft must also state that FY2026's revenue growth is price-led and partly cost-driven, citing the KeyBanc argument that price-led growth deserves a lower multiple than volume-led growth.
- **Calibration:** Hardware medium (the decomposition rests on ONE clean calendar-aligned quarter; the historical decay table uses total-company revenue as an iPhone proxy). App Store medium. Shared open question: the FQ4 FY2026 print — Hardware says Services reaccelerating above 18–20% would weaken the "bounded absorber" conclusion; App Store says any disclosed US commission growth would falsify the erosion claim. Both point at the same forthcoming Services number as decisive.

## Tension 4: Google is one concentrated counterparty across two loci — and the TAC annuity plausibly caused part of the AI failure

- **AI** commits: ORIGIN of the late-follower position is REVEALED, not chosen (high confidence — Federighi's quality-bar admission, an 18-month slip chain, a $250M consumer settlement, Giannandrea's exit); CURRENT ECONOMICS is a capital-efficient but conditional rental structure, centred on ~$1B/yr for Google Gemini powering Siri. Explicit boundary: "a Google decision (post the US v. Google remedies regime) to reprice or restrict the Apple relationship would move this from 'efficient rental' toward 'revealed dependency'."
- **TAC** commits: Google pays Apple ~$23–29B/yr; the remedy bars exclusive Apple–Google generative-AI deals in any Safari mode and forces annual rebidding; the court found Google's payments "disincentivize Apple from launching its own search engine."
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** CONVERGENT CAUSAL LINK PLUS CONCENTRATION. Two findings from separate loci connect causally. The court found the TAC payments disincentivised Apple from building its own search capability; the AI locus found Apple's AI position originated as a failure to build. The largest contingent income stream is therefore plausibly a CONTRIBUTING CAUSE of the late-follower position the reader views as an advantage — Apple was paid, for two decades, not to build the capability it now rents back from the same counterparty. Net, Apple receives roughly $22–28B from Google while paying ~$1B for Gemini: one counterparty sits on both sides of Apple's most court-exposed revenue and its most strategically important AI dependency, under a remedy regime that constrains both relationships at once.
- **How the draft should engage this:** The test of reader prior 1 (heading 5) must present the causal link — that the TAC arrangement plausibly contributed to the late-follower origin — as an inference with its evidence, not an established fact, and the verdict (heading 8) must name Google as a single concentrated counterparty exposure spanning the binary-events and AI sections, rather than treating TAC and the Gemini rental as unrelated.
- **Calibration:** AI high on origin, medium on current economics. TAC medium. The CAUSAL link is the orchestrator's cross-locus inference from two primary findings (court's disincentive finding; Federighi's failure admission). Neither investigator asserted it directly; the draft must label it inference, not finding. What would change it: evidence of a pre-2024 Apple internal strategy document rationalising the AI gap as deliberate "wait for commoditisation" — the AI investigator searched for this and found none.

## Tension 5: Where does the premium come from? — little is flow artefact, so the durability discount bites harder

- **Passive** commits: no Apple-specific passive-flow share of the multiple can be derived from the literature; but two independent mechanisms (the Koijen–Richmond–Yogo demand system's constant per-investor elasticity, which structurally cannot produce a firm-specific multiplier; and Bouchaud's prediction that the multiplier scales with volatility/volume) point to Apple's per-dollar flow multiplier sitting BELOW the aggregate $3–8 (Gabaix–Koijen) / ~14% (KRY) benchmarks — even though Apple's EXPOSURE to mechanical passive ownership is plausibly above the median.
- **TAC + App Store** (via the recomputed falsification test in orchestrator-notes.md) commit: 19.7–28.3% of total gross profit and 46.7–67.2% of Services gross profit sits in repriceable or eroding classes.
- **The cross-locus dynamic:** SYNTHESIS OF THE MULTIPLE DECOMPOSITION. The query's minimum component 2 asks how much of the multiple is earned versus flow/factor artefact. If the passive-flow contribution is small for Apple specifically, more of the premium multiple must be justified by EARNED earnings quality. But Tensions 1–3 show a material slice of the highest-margin earnings is repriceable, eroding, or interface-exposed. A small flow cushion therefore makes the durability discount MORE consequential for the multiple, not less: the premium has less mechanical support to fall back on. The caveat runs the other way in stress: Passive's boundary condition notes larger short-horizon, crisis and factor-unwind effects (the documented June–July 2025 quality unwind), so flow effects matter more exactly when multiples compress.
- **How the draft should engage this:** The multiple-decomposition section (heading 3) must distinguish EXPOSURE (high index weight) from ELASTICITY (below-aggregate price impact per dollar), refuse to present any Apple-specific passive-share number, and connect the result explicitly to the falsification test: a small flow component means the headline multiple rests disproportionately on earnings whose durability the report has just shown to be mixed.
- **Calibration:** Passive medium (high that no Apple-specific number exists; medium on direction, which extrapolates two theoretical mechanisms). Factor crowding is an audited absence for Apple (no source names it) and must not be quantified. What would change it: a demand-system re-estimation broken out by market-cap decile or naming individual mega-caps.

### Tension 1 — DOWNGRADED (medium → low-medium)

Adversarial search for a second data point on AI query migration found NONE.
It found two things that weaken the claim:

1. **Advocacy context.** Eddy Cue's Safari testimony was given in US v. Google,
   where his "primary objective was to convince the judge that the search
   market is already so competitive that he should allow Google to continue
   paying it $20 billion a year" [[stats-from-a-dying-web]]. Apple had a direct
   financial interest in the claim. The original testimony is corroborated only
   as to what was SAID [[google-searches-in-apples-safari-fall-for-first-time-in-22-years]].
2. **Contrary aggregate data.** Google searches grew roughly 20% in 2024 and
   Google handles 373x as many searches as OpenAI's chatbot [[stats-from-a-dying-web]].

**How the draft must now engage it:** present AI query migration as a
PLAUSIBLE, UNCONFIRMED risk to Safari-originated search revenue specifically,
supported by one self-interested data point and contradicted at the aggregate
level — NOT as an established trend "already underway". The narrower claim
survives: TAC is paid on Safari-originated queries, which can fall while total
Google search grows (queries can move into the Google app, AI Overviews or
chatbots). The convergence between the TAC and lock-in investigators is still
worth reporting, but both rested on the same single fact, so it is weaker
convergence than it first appeared. The legal-versus-AI framing of the verdict
must be softened accordingly: courts remain the MORE CERTAIN mechanism; AI
migration is the POTENTIALLY faster one.
