# Which fraction of Apple's earnings is contingent on arrangements a court or regulator can void — and what does that imply for a 2–5 year holding view?

Method: first-principles deconstruction (Gates 0–4.5), all phases completed
Audience: informed technical investor forming a view, not executing a trade

## AUSGANGSSITUATION

The reader wants to understand the investment thesis for Apple over a 2–5 year
horizon well enough to hold and defend an informed view. No trade is attached,
so the report need not end in a buy/sell recommendation — but where credible
sources disagree it MUST adjudicate and commit, with reasoning, rather than
presenting a balanced survey. Four angles are all in scope: fundamentals and
valuation, product and AI strategy, regulatory and legal exposure, and supply
chain and geopolitics. Success: the reader can argue both bull and bear cases
competently, and knows which the evidence actually favours and why.

## Fundamentale Wahrheiten (Phase 1 — Dekonstruktion)

Objectively true, independent of convention:
- A share is a claim on future cash flows to equity holders; nothing else
  ultimately sets its value.
- 2–5y total return decomposes exactly into fundamental growth + multiple
  change + shareholder returns. This is an arithmetic identity, not a theory.
- Buybacks raise EPS with no revenue growth, by shrinking the denominator.
- Higher discount rates compress long-duration cash flows more than short.
- A ~$3–4T company cannot compound at small-cap rates; growth must decay
  toward GDP+ over long horizons.

Necessary vs. conventional:
| Element | Verdict | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| "Apple is a hardware company" | convention | Ecosystem/services framing may fit better now |
| "P/E vs. its own history is the valuation anchor" | convention | Assumes business mix unchanged |
| "The iPhone cycle drives the stock" | convention | Was true; may now be legacy framing |
| "AI capability is existential for Apple" | convention | Widely asserted, rarely argued from first principles |
| Multiple change dominates fundamentals over 2–5y for mega-caps | unsure | Empirical, horizon-dependent |
| Replacement cycles lengthen as marginal improvement shrinks | unsure | AI could reset this |
| Sustained monopoly-like rents attract regulatory attack | unsure | Political-economy regularity, not a law |

## Arbiträre Annahmen (Phase 2 — Annahmen-Check)

| Assumption | Verdict | What would settle it |
|---|---|---|
| The premium multiple is earned by lock-in and capital returns | load-bearing | Decompose the premium net of buyback yield and index flows |
| Passive/index flows mechanically support the multiple | unknown | Passive share of float; mega-cap behaviour in drawdowns |
| Buybacks provide a price floor | arbitrary | Discretionary and pro-cyclical — they shrink when cash flow weakens |
| "Quality factor" crowding is part of the multiple, not the business | unknown | Factor-exposure and drawdown correlation |
| Antitrust remedies and appeals run multi-year | load-bearing | Statutory and appellate timelines are externally fixed |
| Manufacturing diversification is physically slow | load-bearing | Capex-to-output lead times for India/Vietnam capacity |
| AI product cycles resolve inside the window | unknown | Historical lag from platform announcement to revenue impact |
| A multiple re-rating needs years | arbitrary | Re-ratings take weeks; only fundamentals need years |
| Ecosystem lock-in is structural, not switching-cost inertia | unknown | EU DMA markets are a live natural experiment |
| Owning silicon is load-bearing for margin | load-bearing | Margin attribution vs. merchant-silicon competitors |
| Services growth can offset hardware maturity | unknown | Required services CAGR to hold total growth flat |

Two assumptions are the reader's own prior and MUST be tested directly, not
assumed away and not merely restated:

1. **Late-follower status in AI is currently an ADVANTAGE**, because avoiding
   the capex arms race lets Apple allocate capital efficiently and buy or rent
   capability once inference commoditises.
2. **Apple's payments business (Apple Pay, App Store commissions) faces
   near-term disruption from crypto/AI payment rails.**

Note a distinction the report must preserve: the Google search-placement fee
(TAC) is NOT a payment service. Disruption of payment rails does not touch it.
The two must be analysed separately and never conflated.

## Neuaufbau von Null (Phase 3)

Built only from the Phase 1 truths, the thesis is assembled by classifying
earnings by DURABILITY, not by product segment:

| Class | Contents | Valuation treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Annuity-like | Subscriptions, installed-base services | High multiple — genuinely recurring |
| Cyclical | Hardware units × ASP | Lower multiple — replacement-cycle dependent |
| Contingent | App Store commissions | Discounted for regulatory attack |
| Binary | Google TAC | Probability-weighted, not multiplied |

A dollar of App Store commission under active legal challenge is not worth a
dollar of iPhone gross profit, and neither is worth a dollar of Google TAC.
Blending them into one EPS and applying one P/E destroys precisely the
information a 2–5 year view requires.

Minimum necessary components:
1. Earnings decomposed by durability class, not product segment
2. The multiple decomposed into earned vs. passive-flow/factor artefact
3. Binary events listed explicitly with magnitude and timing
4. Required services CAGR to hold total growth flat as hardware matures

Minimum necessary time: long enough to observe the EU DMA natural experiment,
one full AI product cycle, and a first-instance legal ruling. That is what
justifies a 2–5 year horizon — not a round number.

## Implementierung (Phase 4)

| Barrier | Real or convention | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Google TAC and App Store commissions are not disclosed separately | real | Partly surmountable: DOJ–Google discovery put figures on the record |
| Separating earned multiple from passive/factor effects is contested | real | Show a range and its assumptions, not a point estimate |
| Assigning probabilities to legal outcomes | partly real | Remedy DESIGN is analysable even when the ruling is not predictable |
| Sell-side research is organised by segment, not durability | convention | Sourcing instruction: prefer filings and court records over commentary that imports the segment frame |

Cheapest prototype test: build the durability decomposition from one fiscal
year of public data plus court-surfaced figures, and check whether the implied
blended multiple diverges materially from the headline P/E. If it does not, the
frame adds nothing and the conventional view stands. Run this falsification
BEFORE any forecasting.

Disruptive conclusion if followed through: the decisive question is not "is
Apple's AI good enough" — where nearly all published attention sits — but "what
fraction of earnings is contingent on arrangements a court or regulator can
void?" If that fraction is large, Apple is a maturing hardware business with a
large contingent annuity attached, deserving a materially different multiple.
If small, the bear case is noise and the premium is earned.

## Research questions

Most decision-critical first:

1. What fraction of Apple's earnings falls into each durability class
   (annuity / cyclical / contingent / binary)? Quantify with ranges and state
   the estimation method where disclosure is absent.
2. Google TAC: what remedy can a court actually compel, on what timetable, and
   what would the arrangement be worth under each plausible remedy design?
3. Is late-follower status in AI currently an advantage, as the reader argues?
   Weigh capital-allocation efficiency against the risk that AI relocates the
   user interface away from the device. Commit to a position.
4. Are Apple Pay and App Store commissions genuinely exposed to near-term
   disruption from crypto/AI payment rails, as the reader argues? Give adoption
   evidence and a 2–5 year timetable, or refute it.
5. How much of Apple's multiple is earned versus passive-flow and quality-factor
   artefact? Range, with method stated.
6. Is ecosystem lock-in structural, or switching-cost inertia that regulation
   can unwind? Use EU DMA markets as the natural experiment.
7. What services CAGR is required to hold total growth flat as hardware matures,
   and is that rate historically plausible?
8. Over 2–5y specifically, does multiple change dominate fundamental change for
   mega-caps? Evidence, not assertion.
9. Does an AI-driven upgrade supercycle plausibly reset the replacement cycle,
   or is lengthening structural?
10. How much of Apple's gross margin is attributable to owning silicon, versus
    competitors buying merchant silicon?

## Scope boundaries

In scope: the four angles named above; primary sources (10-K/10-Q filings, court
records, regulatory documents) weighted over analyst commentary; explicit
adjudication of contested points.

Out of scope: price targets; quarterly earnings estimates; peer-multiple
comparison as a valuation anchor (rejected at Phase 1 as convention that imports
another company's durability mix); personalised investment advice or a
buy/sell/hold recommendation.

Non-negotiable: where sources conflict, the report commits to a position and
defends it. An even-handed survey that leaves adjudication to the reader is a
failed report.

## What a good answer looks like

The reader can argue both bull and bear cases competently and knows which the
evidence favours. Their two priors (late-follower advantage; payment-rail
disruption) have been tested against evidence and either supported or refuted
with reasons — not politely accommodated. The durability decomposition is
quantified well enough to see whether the headline multiple is misleading. What
would make it useless: restating consensus, hedging on every contested point, or
conflating the Google search fee with payment services.
